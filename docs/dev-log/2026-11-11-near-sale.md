# 2026-11-11 — 0351: a best sale she can pay for (`near-sale`, docs/SUCCESS_PLAN.md slice T5, owner row 314)

## The order
`world.best_sales(fleet, port)` (0348, re-cut by 0350) names, for every good, the sale with the biggest TOTAL profit at the
units her hold takes. For a new company at Lisbon with 40,000 coins that is Matsumae, Hagatna, Salvador: weeks at sea, hours
of real time; most cards cost more than her purse and open a tray that says "No money"; the card shows no time and no cost;
and the hop to Setubal that pays is never named. The slice: a best sale a new player can use.

## The rule, in two sentences
For every good the card names the sale with the **best profit per day among the sales her purse can buy** — where she lies,
at the units the docked tray's own Max allows (`public.fleet_buy_capacity`: her hold, the city's stock, the day's cap, her
purse), a sale counting when the buy (and her stores for the leg, unless they may be owed) is within her coins. The dearest
of those same sales is served beside it as the far choice, and a good with sales priced but none within her purse says
"No money".

## The cap authority's walk, bisected (the second re-cut body; added after the first cut of this PR measured +24 %)
The first cut asked `public.fleet_buy_capacity` as it stood and was slower for exactly the company it is for: the walk made
about 136 quotes for Lisbon's ten goods at a 40,000 purse, on top of the ~160 the rest of a call makes. The lead's order:
bisect the walk inside the one authority, with the same answers proven.

**Why the walk stepped by ten.** Read from its history: `fleet_buy_capacity` was made in 0007 and re-made once in 0017
(to pass the fleet so her purser's price is the one quoted); 0061-0273 only call it. 0007's header: a maximum worked out
from room alone offered more than her purse could pay, "so it walks down in `trade_step_tuns` steps and asks
`world.quote()`", the function the trade itself uses. The step is the price walk's resolution (0207: "the RESOLUTION of the
price walk, not a quantity a trade may move (any count above nought is accepted)"), and the one-tun tail was added in the
same body so a dear good is not answered "0" ("she could afford two diamonds and the picker would offer her none"). So the
step protects nothing but time: there is no lot of ten, no tie rule. The rule is: **the first count, in the walk's order,
whose quoted total is within her purse**, where the order is the structural limit, then every whole step below it, then
(below one step) one tun at a time. The walk's answer is therefore NOT always the largest count she can pay for (it may sit
up to nine tuns under it); the bisect keeps exactly that.

**What the bisect does.** It numbers those same counts in that same order and finds the first one within her purse by
searching instead of walking: the limit itself (one quote: the whole answer when her hold, the stock or the day's cap
binds), then a start where her purse would land if every tun cost what the limit's did, doubling strides from there, then
halving. Where the search starts changes how many quotes are asked, never the answer. It is the walk's answer wherever a
larger count never costs less.

**Is that so? Proven three ways, none of them by argument alone.**
1. *The premise, read whole* (`cards.sql`, local): `world.quote`'s buy total at EVERY count from 1 to what her hold takes,
   every good at Lisbon: 2,634 counts, the total falls as the count rises 0 times, with the price press dark and with it
   lit (the two switches set inside the scene, rolled back). No counter-example could be constructed.
2. *The walk against the bisect* (inside the migration, on its rolled-back probe): a `pg_temp` copy of the body before the
   file and one of the body after it, each with the same four lines swapped so the probe can set her purse, the stock, the
   day's cap, the step and the harbour without writing a purse, a market row or a knob. **460 pairs in 88 cells, every pair
   equal** (max_qty, est_total, bound_by):
   - where she lies, nothing set (the copy is also held equal to the real body), then purses 40,000 / 4,000,000 / 400,000 /
     5,000 / 500 / 1 / 0, every good (80 pairs);
   - the stock forced to 7, 55 and 12, the day's cap to 3, 25 and 9, under purses 4,000,000 / 40,000 / 500 (150 pairs);
   - the edge: a purse of exactly what 20, 7 and 1 of a good cost, found down the walk and as the limit itself (54 pairs;
     18 answers cost her whole purse to the coin);
   - another harbour (Setubal's goods, the seven purses; 28 pairs);
   - other steps than the knob's (7, 2.5, 1, 25) with the limit above and below a step, and a step of one tun over the
     whole hold (108 pairs);
   - cargo aboard (her hold smaller, her purse lighter): her own purse and three set ones (40 pairs).
   Positive controls: each of hold, purse, stock and day's cap bound somewhere in the matrix.
3. *Mutants* (local, `measure.mjs --apply=<mutant>`): 12 breakages of the bisect, one line each. 10 are red on the matrix
   (a wrong count formula, a wrong tail, `<` for `<=` at the limit and inside the search, the answer's quote not kept, a
   start not clamped, a search that stops after three strides or does not halve). 2 survive and are the same function:
   a different stride length, and "the last place is not asked" (`v_hi := v_n` for `v_n + 1`), which can only differ from
   the walk if the search's start stands BEFORE an answer at the last place, which needs a larger count to cost less.

**Who asks the cap authority** (a `pg_proc` scan; the migration asserts the list is exactly this):

## Decided by default (the owner or the lead may reverse each)
1. **The one cap authority, made fast rather than copied.** `public.fleet_buy_capacity` is asked as the one authority,
   and its own walk is bisected inside it (the lead's order after the first cut); no cap rule is written in `best_sales`.
   The search keeps the walk's answer exactly, including that it is the first count of the walk's own steps within her
   purse and not always the very largest count she could pay for.
2. **Profit per day is profit per real second to the sale**, with no port time, no return leg and no floor: a 10-second
   hop that gains 2,000 beats a 57-minute voyage that gains 35,000. The far choice is there for the other taste.
3. **The far choice is the dearest sale WITHIN HER PURSE**, at the same purse-capped units, not the hold-full figure the
   card named before (which she could not buy). For a company whose hold binds, it is the old answer to the coin.
4. **Anything but her hold binding the Max (purse, stock, the day's cap) prices the card at the Max**, not only the purse:
   the card never asks for more than the tray will sell her.
5. **Her stores count against her purse unless they may be owed** (`public.credit_on`), as the trip tray judges "No money".
6. **From a city she does not lie at, the units are not purse-capped** (no authority answers there); sales past her purse
   are dropped, and a good left with none says "No money". Locally 3 of Setubal's 4 goods read "No money" for a 40,000
   purse seen from Lisbon. Honest, and less useful than the home board.
7. **The card shows the time, not the cost.** Four figures do not fit a 140-px card; the cost is the tray's Total, one
   press away, and in the line's title.
8. **The far choice is a row in the tray, not a second line on the card** (the card is already two lines at 390 px).
9. **Her cargo's card** follows the same rule (best per day, far served) but the tray shows no far row for it: Cargo
   already lists where her cargo sells best.
10. **The branch carries a merge of main** (0350 is on main, not on `full-names`), so the PR's diff against main shows
    #589 and #590 too until they merge.
