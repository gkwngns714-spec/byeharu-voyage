## The map: a name stands at its own city

The lead, reading this pass's own picture of a sea on a phone: "Barcelona" stood beside Lisbon, "Bordeaux" off Galicia, "Genoa"
over France, and Valencia, Algiers, Malaga and Tunis in a column off Spain. Very likely the owner's "weird".

**Cause.** The planner set a name to the right of its mark, then the left, then over and under, and when all eight snug places
were taken it stepped the name out one or two rings (21 and 34 px) on a hairline. On a coast that is right: a name is 80 px
and the coast is the whole glass. On a phone holding a sea, 80 px is eight degrees: Iberia is 110 px wide, so a name set to
the left of Barcelona's mark lies across Portugal, and the hairline of a stepped-out name cannot be seen on the day land.
Every name was within its allowed distance of its mark, and the picture was still wrong geography.

**Rule** (`src/chart/labels.ts` `centredOnMark`, set by `ChartCanvas.tsx` past a coast, the density scale's own start:
`WIDE_SCALE.fromUnitsPerPx`):

1. A harbour's name is set **over its mark, or under it**, centred on it. Not to a side, not on a diagonal.
2. **Nothing steps out.** A name with no place at its own mark is not drawn at this zoom; it comes back as the glass comes in.
3. **The lesser name is the one that goes.** Names are placed in the order of their rank (a harbour your fleet is using, then by
   size), and a name gives way only to the marks of its equals and betters, so a great harbour's name is not lost to a
   roadstead's dot beside it. The harbour left without a name is a quiet dot (the first pass's rule).
4. A harbour a fleet of yours is using may still take a side when over and under are taken: it never loses its name.
5. A fleet's own name keeps the usual order: she is on open water and her name rides beside her.

On a coast (a phone holding 20 degrees or less) nothing changed: the eight sides, the rings and the hairlines, which the owner
asked for on 2026-10-20 to read every city of a coast.

**Cost, said plainly:** a sea on a phone names fewer harbours than before (western Europe at 41 %: about eleven, was about
twenty). Each one that is named is where its city is.

**Law** (`tests/map.look.spec.ts`): a pure test of the rule (over or under only, the middle of the name on its mark's meridian,
the least name dropped, no leader, the rule off on a coast); and on the running chart at 390 and 320 px, at a sea and furthest
out, every harbour name is over or under and its middle is within 26 px of its own mark (one name at most may take a side: the
harbour your fleet lies in).

**The fight's ring** was a 2-px line whose pulse fell to 45 %, pale on the day land. It is 2.5 px, its pulse stops at 70 %, and
its wash is 15 % (was 10).
