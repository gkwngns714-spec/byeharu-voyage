# 2026-10-19 — every market is ten times deeper (migration 0208, `osn-trade-qty-x10`)

**The order.** The owner, 2026-10-19, verbatim: *"make the trade good quantity 10 times larger for all"*.

**Decided by default (the owner may reverse it).** Every city market's goods stock and its normal (target) stock
become 10× larger, for every good in every city. The flows that refill them (production, recovery toward
normal) and the per-day trade cap scale 10× with them, so a market refills at the same pace relative to its
size. Prices stay where they are. Ship holds, warehouses, the 10-unit price step, the route probe (50) and the
contract sizes (10–30) do not change.

**Version.** 0208 at writing: prod head 0205 (the good switch); 0206 is the good-board fix-forward (PR #264) and 0207 is `osn-arrivals-fast`, both deploying first. It renumbers to
the prod head + 1 at push.

## 1. Every place that sets or limits a market quantity (read on prod, 2026-10-19)

I dumped every function body in `public`, `world`, `cmd` and `voyage` from prod (507 functions) and read
each one that touches `port_goods`, `stock`, `stock_target`, `production_rate` or `trade_daily`. I also read
all of `world_config`.

A market row stores three quantities: `stock`, `stock_target` and `production_rate`. Every reader works from
those three, so scaling them scales everything below. No function body needs to change.

| what | where | how it reacts to × 10 |
|---|---|---|
| price | `world.mid_from_terms`: `power(stock_target / greatest(stock, 1), elasticity)` | stock / target is unchanged, so the price is unchanged |
| price walk | `world.quote_priced`: 10-unit steps, each priced at the moved stock | the first step is unchanged; a large order slips a tenth as much |
| recovery and production | `public.stock_after` (composed by `tick_market_drift` and `price_path`) | this formula is linear in stock, target and production, so × 10 in gives × 10 out |
| per-day trade cap | `world.daily_cap_remaining` = 0.35 × `stock_target` × skill × works | × 10 |
| glut | `cmd.do_sell`: + 0.30 × units / `stock_target` | the same sale leaves a tenth of the mark |
| NPC merchants | `npc_leg_lines`, `standing_route_lines`, `npc_affordable`, `npc_buy_ceiling` | no constant of their own; parcels are capped by the day caps (× 10), the purse and the hold |
| buy maximum | `public.fleet_buy_capacity` = min(hold, stock, day cap), then the purse | built from the other rules; nothing to scale |
| contracts | `public.contract_draw`: 1–3 steps of 10, never above the day cap | kept: a delivery sized to a hold |
| route probe | `route_probe_tuns` 50 (`world.trade_routes` with no fleet) | kept: it stands in for a fleet's hold |
| price step | `trade_step_tuns` 10 | kept: it is the walk's resolution; any count is accepted |
| holds and storage | hull capacities, `warehouse_tuns_per_tier` 150 | kept, as ordered. No code treats a trade cap as a hold cap |
| chart stock line | `price_history.stock` (47 slots, 63,356 rows) | scaled × 10 so the line does not jump at the deploy; `mid` untouched |
| today's traded units | `trade_daily.qty` | a record of real trades, so it is untouched |

**The one authority.** The seed formula `greatest(60, round(200 × size_tier × (1.60 − affinity)))` (production:
5 % of it where affinity < 0.80) was written out in seven migrations (0005, 0041, 0048, 0058, 0062, 0065, 0090),
in the world-growth generator and in the balance tuner. That is spaghetti, and it is folded here:

- the knob is `market_depth` = 10 in `world_config`;
- `world.market_depth_at(size_tier, affinity, depth)` is that formula × depth. It is immutable, takes only its
  arguments (like `world.affinity_at`) and has no client grant;
- callers: 0208 itself; `scripts/build-world-growth.mjs`, which composes it whenever the baseline has it (a
  future world growth seeds new rows 10× deep and asserts that); and `scripts/db/tune-balance.mjs`.

Like the affinity knobs, the depth applies when rows are written. Changing the knob alone moves no row, and its
description says so.

## 2. The price-unchanged proof

**On prod, read-only (`begin transaction read only`; every function called has provolatile `s` or `i`):**

- the pre-image guard passes: 132,090 rows, and 0 of them are off 0005's seed depth (target and production both);
- all 132,090 rows priced through the price expression (`world.mid_from_terms`, immutable) at (10 × target,
  10 × stock) against today: **0 move**;
- the probe set (every 7th row a quay offers, by port and good code) has 193 rows. All 193 are priced, all have
  stock > 0 (the lowest is 116.36) and a positive day cap, and 162 fill a 200-unit buy today;
- the lowest stock on any prod row is ≥ 10 (100 after × 10), so the 1-unit floor in the price never binds.
  Below one unit (the bottom 10 units after × 10) an emptied market's price would read higher than the old
  equivalent, up to the band ceiling. That case is not present on prod.

**In the migration's self-assert (run in the transaction that applies it):** world.price's mid, ask and bid,
and world.quote for 1 unit and for 10 units (one step), buy and sell, are recorded before the update and again
after it, on the same probe set. They must be equal to the cent, while each probe row's stock is exactly 10×.
See §3 for the local run.

### What the depth does to a first voyage (proof 05): an owner-visible consequence

Proof 05 measures the median best first voyage on a pinned market. The market is now deeper, so the same stake
walks the price a tenth as far, and the voyage keeps more of the gap. Measured: **19.4 % on main at 0204**
(CI run 39184962583) became **21.1 % with 0208** (per-market medians {20.7, 22.7, 20.2}, local). That is
outside the old 13.0–20.0 band. I moved the band deliberately, by the measured +1.7, to 14.7–21.7. Its width
(7.0) is unchanged, so it still refuses the same size of error in drift_sigma, and a dated comment on the line
says why. **For the owner:** starters earn about 1.7 points more per voyage, and large orders slip less
everywhere. That is what "10× larger" does to a market. Reverse it if it is not wanted.

## 4. Client

Every stock display reads a ratio: `stockLevel` / `stockFillPct` (stock ÷ target), `StockGauge`, the chart's
stock line (`priceChartModel`, track 0 … 2 × normal) and `world.market`'s `stock_band`. The figures print
through `formatInt` (`6,700 / 6,700 units`). The tray's maxima are served (`fleet_buy_capacity.max_qty`). No
client clamp has a size baked in. **No client change.**

**One consequence for the owner to judge.** The trade tray's slider spans the stock on the quay, as asked in
OWNER_REQUESTS row 63 (*"the gauge max will be the stock it has in the market"*), and its tick marks what the
hold allows. At 10× stock, a 60-unit hold sits at about 1 % of a 6,000-unit track. So dragging is coarse:
roughly 20 units per pixel on a phone. Typing the number, − / +, the wheel, PageUp/PageDown and the "lot" and
"max" chips all still work exactly. I did not change it. Changing what the slider spans would reverse an owner
request, so that is the owner's call.
