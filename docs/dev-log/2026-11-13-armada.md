## The order

Owner row 323 (2026-11-13): *"with more than one fleet, max 6 fleet - an armada."* The lead's reading, decided by default: a named group of two to six of one company's fleets that gather, sail on one order at the slowest fleet's speed, arrive together, and meet a fight as one side. Not a change to how many ships a fleet holds; no player fights a player.

**Nothing here was run on production, nothing was read from production, and no existing switch or knob was touched.** Everything below was measured on the local PGlite world, from the cached prefix at head 0356.

## The raid, measured

`.scratch` scene (not committed): `voyage.raid_report` over 150 seeds a row, sides built from real `voyage.fleet_hulls` of probe fleets, repeated to make 1, 2, 4 and 6 fleets. A trader fleet is 2 barcas (escort score 6.32), a war fleet 2 byeong (25.24). R is what one fleet draws alone. Scale 0 is one squadron whatever her size; scale 1 is the knob as shipped.

**The finding:** the raiders of an anonymous raid are sized by the sea alone, not by cargo. Unscaled, four gunless trader fleets never lose anything at any danger measured: a free pass. So the knob ships at 1: an armada draws the raiders her fleets would have drawn apart, and what she gains is that a war fleet fights for a trader fleet (one covers about one). The owner may turn `armada_raid_scale` down to make armadas safer, or up.

**Plunder across holds** (proof 68, one run): on one PLUNDERED day the lead lost 14 of 40 and 3 of 9 (35 %), her sister 5 of 21 (24 %). Each hold is searched on its own day roll, 25 to 50 %.

## Lit, and what that means today

Lit (`armada_enabled` true), because the owner asked for it. The risks, each bounded:

- **Raids made trivially safe:** bounded by `armada_raid_scale` 1 (above).
- **Reach:** while `fleet_room_bite` is off no player can start a second fleet (`cmd.move_ship` refuses `E_NOT_IN_USE`). So on prod an armada is reachable only by a company that already holds two fleets. `docs/FLEETS_AND_SLOTS.md` read every human company at one fleet on 2026-10-25; `before.sql` reads it again. If that still holds, **nobody can form an armada until the owner lights fleet room**.
- **Every fleet of the world pays one probe:** a speed read, a hazard roll and a raid day each read one more primary key of an empty table. The migration's own notice, on 60 fleets interleaved: OLD 0.58 to 0.62 ms a speed read, NEW 0.45 to 0.48 ms (the difference is noise; the probe is not measurable against the formula).
- **Forward rollback:** `armada_enabled` false. The whole slice: `scripts/ops/harness/armada/rollback-0367.sql`, tested locally: after it the six re-cut bodies and the speed are main's to the byte, 956 functions as before, no table and no knob left.

**The owner's knobs, for the owner:** six fleets in an armada needs a company that holds six. `fleet_hard_max` is 5 and, with `fleet_room_bite` off, a company holds 2. Neither was touched. An armada is 2 fleets today; 5 with the room lit; 6 only if the owner raises `fleet_hard_max` to 6 (and adds a sixth step to the fleet ladder). `armada_fleet_max` 6 waits for that.

### Found on the way

- `cmd.figures` refuses a have above a need. The first draft gave `E_ARMADA_SIZE` figures for seven fleets against six and raised instead of refusing. Proof 68 caught it; too many fleets now carries no bar.
- Proof 68 failed once in four runs before it was made to hold under any dice: its arrival check required both fleets `DOCKED`, and a raid can leave a flagship with no hull, in which case `voyage.settle` lands her `UNABLE_TO_SAIL`. That is the read of the cause, not a captured trace: the failing run printed no detail. The check now reads both states; 9 runs green since (6 on main's chain, 3 on the stacked chain).
- New hulls leave the yard with casks for 15 days, so a short passage costs an armada nothing. `cost` is 0 there and that is right.

## Not proven
