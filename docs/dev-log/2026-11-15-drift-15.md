# 2026-11-15 drift-15: every harbour is repriced every fifteen minutes (migration 0420)

**The owner (2026-11-15, verbatim):** "market price rewritten - all 156,621 - every 15 minute would be good".
**Decided (the lead):** every harbour every 15 REAL minutes, spread evenly (one fifteenth of the harbours each minute), not all 156,621 rows on
the quarter hour: a spike every 15 minutes is what `docs/DATABASE_BUDGET.md` forbids.

## What decides how often a harbour is repriced (read, not assumed)
- `public.drift_ports_due(t)` (0409) gives harbour number k (port order by id) when `k mod n = floor(epoch(t) / 60) mod n`, n = `drift_spread_runs`.
  So a harbour's turn comes every **n real minutes**: the job's one-minute cadence times n. The slot length does not enter it.
- `drift_slot_seconds` (300 on production since 0413) decides only how far one turn moves the walk: a row k slots behind takes ONE compounded
  step with the law of k one-slot steps (0409). At n = 15 a turn is exactly 900 / 300 = 3 slots on, so every turn writes every row of the harbour.
- Production before 0420: n = 50 (0409's 10, x 5 by 0413's door at the switch to the one-hour day): every 50 minutes, ~3,130 rows a run.

## What 0420 does
1. `drift_spread_runs` 50 -> 15 (refuses from any other value; the description records 50).
2. **The door no longer moves the spread.** `public.clock_set` multiplied it by old / new so a harbour kept the same number of slots between
   turns. Since 0409's compounded step the walk per voyage-day does not depend on the spread, so that scaling bought nothing, and it would undo
   the owner's 15 minutes the next time the clock moves: the 0413 rollback (`clock_set(960)`) would have set it to 3, ~52,000 rows a minute.
   The spread is now a pace in real minutes, like every cron cadence (0413's "STAYS AS IT IS"). Two hunks in `public.clock_set`, each once and
   reversing. Proof 110 (DH_RATES) and proof 111 (its reset to the old clock) are adjusted to it; proof 117 holds the new rule.

## Prices move as far per voyage-day as before
`drift_sigma`, `drift_theta`, `drift_clamp`, `drift_slot_seconds` are untouched. Proof 117 on production's 300 s slot: with no noise, four
15-minute turns and one turn after the hour both give theta^12 x 0.1 = 0.0282; with the noise, 1,887 rows from drift 0 after four turns spread
0.0442 against the twelve-step law 0.0440. The 0420 self-assert does the no-noise check on the slot it finds (on production: 3 slots a turn, 12 an hour).

## The load (the budget)
- ~156,621 / 15 = **~10,400 port_goods rows a run**, every minute, evenly: +7,300 rows a minute over 0409's ~3,130 (the world before 0409 wrote
  156,621 a minute). Over the budget's 5,000 rows a run by the owner's order; the job's schedule is unchanged.
- On this chain (159,766 rows) the fifteen runs reach 9,435 to 11,322 rows each (harbours differ in size), 17 or 18 harbours a run.
- The lead reads `db-health/gate.sql` before the deploy.

## Seen, not built

most a few slots behind. A cap of k at a few hundred would close it; not in this slice.
