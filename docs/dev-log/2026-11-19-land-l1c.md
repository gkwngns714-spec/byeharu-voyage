## The market guard (0457's rule) over all four

One authority: `scripts/build-land-migration.mjs`, its new foot "L1c".
- **Repeat** with no idle time is trips back to back: the pace the guard already counts. Off only adds idle time.
- **Mules:** an hour is `p x 3600 / (2 (s + m) + p x turn)`, under `3600 / turn` for every p with a march, so the guard on the turn holds
  with any number of mules (`fastestHourPacked`). A mule adds haul a trip and some an hour, never over the hundredth.
- **Finds:** a find of gem G is held to G's own hundredth: `3600 / the fastest turn / one_in` against G's least middling target
  (`findOneIn` raises one_in until it holds). With the base 1 in 100 it never had to: every one_in is 100.

Measured (chain's world = production; prod read 2026-11-19 with `before-0458.sql`):
- **Every job-region with the top mule** (30 packs, the least march 15 s, Mining 100, the best tool, the crowd's full chance): 231
  job-regions, the tightest **clay at 0.9998** of the hundredth (JAP in the chain, SOU on prod; 0457's turn bound alone was 0.9999 opals).
- **Every find-region:** 182, the most **0.0688** of the hundredth (diamonds off saltpetre, SOU, target 150.75).
- The check can fail (proof 161 L1C_GUARD): a ruby on every turn of tin is **5.7x** the hundredth.
- What a mule does, Lisbon at Mining 1, no tool (0457's rows): **Sea Coal by Plymouth** 6 packs 3,700 s a trip = 5.84 an hour; 30 packs
  10,780 s = **10.02 an hour** (the turn's bound at that level 12.2). **Iron at home** 6 packs 3,900 s = 5.54 an hour; 30 packs 19,380 s
  = 5.57 an hour. So the mule is what the design says it is: it matters on a far trip, hardly at home.
- 0458 applies with its self-assert in **516 ms** (budget 15 s). Proof 161 in 0.7 s; proofs 158 (9/9) and 160 (5/5) unchanged.
- The self-assert's own Baltic probe: lead, 30 packs, 150 turns in 12 h, **1 amber** found and booked (the pinned dice).

## Decided by default (the owner may reverse any)

1. **A find is a gem of the skill's own ladder** (catalogue category `gems`, 19 of Mining's 57) **whose land is the land worked**, never
   the job's own good: 167 job-gem pairs. A good is found only where it lies, as it is dug only there (design 10 no. 2). So **no find
   comes from a land that holds no gem: Iberia and the British Isles among them** (a Lisbon company sees finds only on a far trip,
   e.g. copper by the Maghreb, onyx). The design says "rare finds on every job"; its own geography rule wins here.
2. **One in 100 a turn for each gem**, Melvor's figure (design 2.1). A land with three gems finds one about 3 turns in 100.
3. **No level gate on a find**, no experience for it, and the crowd's second unit is the job's good only, never a second find.
4. **One die a turn**, the first find whose share of [0, 1) holds it (ordered by the ladder): booked in twelve presses or in one, the
   same finds (proof 161).
5. **A find that does not fit waits with the party** (`found_waiting`), like a haul, and the party is not sent again until it is in.
6. **A mule bought while out changes the next Send, not the job out** (terms frozen at Send, design 6.5).
7. **Repeat off while out makes the trip under way the last**; Repeat never undoes Call home. A null answer is `E_BAD_ORDER`.
8. **All four mules bought** refuses with the existing `E_TOP_GRADE` ("Already top grade."), no new code.
9. **The While away line is what is home and not collected**, as `world.land` serves it (turns home and not booked, plus what waited),
   not a difference against the device's mark: the land keeps its own count until Collect. Finds waiting are not on that line.
   The read is asked once per away return, and only while the world serves the land lit.
