## The owner's question: keeping the per-city goods statistics longer

**It is cheap, and 0416 keeps 30 days.** What costs is not the days but the grain.

- The table holds one row per city per good it sells (or that traded there) per count: about **1,270 pairs**.
- A **daily** row of every pair is 1,270 rows a day: **about 0.3 MB a day** with its two indexes (a row is about 140 bytes of heap and
  85 of index).
- The table's bulk is the fine tier (26 hours of counts: 132,000 rows, about 30 MB) and was the hourly tier (5 more days of hours:
  150,000 rows, about 34 MB), which **no screen reads**.

So 0416 folds to a day after 2 days instead of 7 (`stats_city_good_hour_days` 7 to 2) and keeps the days (`stats_city_good_keep_days`
3 to 30). The table, once rewritten:

| kept | rows | size | of which daily rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0409's rule: 3 days, hours kept to day 7 | 190,000 | 43 MB | none |
| 7 days | 169,000 | 38 MB | 6,000 rows, 1.4 MB |
| **30 days (0416)** | **198,000** | **45 MB** | 36,000 rows, 8 MB |
| 90 days | 274,000 | 62 MB | 112,000 rows, 25 MB |
| 365 days | 623,000 | 140 MB | 461,000 rows, 104 MB |

Pages the Stats screens read: the same at every keep. A city's page reads its goods' newest 24 hours through the primary key; older
days are pages nothing touches until something asks for them, and cold pages cost disk, not memory.

**What the screens show from it today: nothing older than a day.** A city's page reads the last 24 hours (what it sold and bought
most, each good's sold and bought, its price a day ago, a 24-point spark), and the price's "why" line reads 24 hours. No graph draws a
city's goods over a week or a month from this table. (A good's PRICE at a city over a week, a month and a year is a different record,
`price_history_hour` / `price_history_day`, and is kept.) So "longer" buys the owner the record, for a graph of what each city sold and
bought of each good by day, when one is built: 30 days are there for it at 8 MB. A year would be 104 MB: say when it is wanted.

## The laps: newest ten, no hours

A merchant closes about 68 laps a day, so ten are about three and a half hours. 0409 kept 26 hours (about 65,000 rows) because four
readers read more than ten:

| reader | read | now reads |
|---|---|---|
| `public.route_earnings` (the fleet card's "today") | the lap rows of 24 hours | `public.route_day` |
| `world.fleet_trades` (the card's day: laps, sold, bought, cargo in and out, net) | the lap rows of 24 hours | `public.route_day` for the totals; the lap rows for the laps listed and the goods by harbour, as before (only the newest 7 ever carried their trades) |
| `public.stats_merchants_window` (the Stats tab's merchants: profit a day, laps, losing; and its "minutes since the count") | the lap rows of the window | whole hours from the hour book, part-hours from the lap rows |
| `public.npc_share_budget` (what a merchant may put into shares) | the dearest of her newest 20 laps inside 3 days | the dearest lap of the last `npc_invest_lap_hours` (8), from the hour book |

The readers within ten are untouched: the card's seven laps (it reads 8), the average of the newest seven, the two laps
`npc_leg_lines` reads, the one the close reads.

**The hour book**, `public.route_hour_laps`: one row per route per hour a lap closed in, with the laps' figures summed. About 900 rows
an hour, kept 80 hours (72,000 rows, about 12 MB). Its one writer is a trigger on the lap table: a lap that closes is added to its
hour in the same transaction. `cmd.standing_route_close_lap` is not touched (0410 and 0411 re-cut it); the close pays one small
upsert more. A deleted lap stays in the book: that is the point of it.

**One reading**, `public.route_day(route, from, to)`: the hours wholly inside the window from the book, plus the laps of the window's
two part-hours from the lap rows still kept. Wherever those rows are kept it is exact, lap for lap: a player's route (100 laps), and
any window inside a merchant's ten (the Stats tab's "minutes since"). For a merchant's 24 hours the oldest part-hour's laps are gone,
so **her "today" is the last 23 to 24 hours**. That is the one thing a player can see, and only on a merchant's card.

The share budget is a rule of the merchants' play, not a screen: "the dearest of her newest 20 laps, none older than 3 days" becomes
"the dearest lap of the last 8 hours". Twenty laps are about seven hours of a merchant's sailing, so it is the same lap nearly always;
a merchant laid up for 8 hours now keeps nothing back for laps (she did after 3 days).

The book is filled once in the migration from the lap rows that stand, so every figure is right from the first minute. That fill
reads the lap table once: seconds if the RUNBOOK's rewrite of it has run, up to a minute on the 226 MB heap of today.

Proof 112: the same staged laps read before and after the cut. Before, every figure equals the staged rows exactly. After (41 laps cut
to 10), every figure equals the truth less the laps of the oldest part-hour and nothing else, in `route_earnings`, on the card and in
the Stats window; the short windows are exact; a player's route is untouched and exact.

Sizes after 0416 and a rewrite: standing_route_laps about 60 MB to about 10 MB (11,000 rows); the hour book 12 MB.
