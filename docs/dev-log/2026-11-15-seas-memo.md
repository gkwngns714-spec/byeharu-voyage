# The pair table keeps step with the courses (2026-11-15, migration 0442, owner row 352)

Two proofs went red on main once 0440 and 0441 were merged (seen on #733's pglite-gate, 2 of 119 files):

- **Proof 98** (`ROW_HOLDS_COURSE`): 18 of 1,141 remembered courses cross a sea their pair's row does not hold.
- **Proof 97** (`TAG_DARK`, at Sakai): the board's tag for three goods sold at Galle is not the plain rule's reading.

Both proofs are right. The table they read was stale. Neither proof's law is changed.

## What the table is

`public.sea_leg_asks` holds, for every two harbours, the seas between them and what the leg asks (title, wave). Trade's board (`public.sea_routes`), the server's pathfinder (`voyage.reach_passage`) and `public.sea_floor` read it. Since 0403 its author is a build, `scripts/build-sea-legs.mjs`: it plots the course the browser plots between the two roadsteads, asks the door's sampler (`public.course_zones`) for its seas, and writes `data/sea-legs.json`. A row also holds the seas of any course the world remembers on the pair (`public.npc_courses`). 0403 says a slice that moves a roadstead, the raster or a harbour re-runs the build.

## The cause: 0440, for both

Measured on the cached world images, with proof 98's own query:

| chain at | lanes off their row | own rows off |
|---|---|---|
| 0439 | 0 | 0 |
| 0440 | 18 | 1 (Galle) |
| 0441 | 18 (the same) | 1 (Galle) |

- 0440 re-sailed the lanes off the drawn land, moved 69 roadsteads and opened three straits. It did not re-run the build.
  - 18 re-sailed lanes now cross a sea their row does not name (Jeju, Galle, Malindi and Tromso among them): the row asks less than the lane.
  - Galle's roadstead moved from the Laccadive Sea (LAC) into the East Indian Ocean (IND-E). Its own row still said LAC, so every leg to Galle asked what the wrong sea asks. That is proof 97's red.
- 0441 broke no row. It changed the search's rule, so what the browser plots differs for some pairs; the new seed covers that.

**On production** (read only, 2026-11-15, head 0441, gate green, beat mean 155 ms; `scripts/ops/harness/seas-memo/before.sql`): 24 lanes off their row (the same 18, and 6 that are the return of one of them and exist only on prod), Galle's own row off, 0 memo rows missing, 64,516 pairs. So on those pairs, and on every leg to Galle, Trade could list a harbour the sail door then refuses, or hide one it opens.

## The fix, migration 0442

`20260818000442_the_pair_table_keeps_step_with_the_courses.sql`, written by `scripts/build-sea-legs-migration.mjs` from `scripts/sea-legs.sql.mjs`. Data and one new function. No hunk in a live body, no knob, no table, no cron job.

1. **`public.sea_leg_asks_keep()`**, a new function (migrations only; no player may call it). It restores the three laws that need no plotting, and says how many rows each moved:
   - a harbour's own row names the sea its roadstead lies in;
   - a row holds every sea of the course the world remembers on its pair;
   - wave and title are the most the row's seas and the destination's own sea ask.

   It adds seas and never removes one.
2. **The table re-shipped from a new build.** The build now plots as the browser's course author does since 0440 (the search, then the re-sailing off the coast mask), which is the course the browser sends the door. 64,262 courses on 12 threads: 4.4 minutes to plot, 11.2 minutes to sample. 3,886 distinct sets of seas. The chain's 1,141 lanes add no sea the plotted courses lack.
3. **`sea_leg_asks_keep()` is then called**, so production's own lanes (it holds 1,351) are in their rows too.

On the chain's world: 2,174 of 64,516 rows change their seas; 86 change what they ask (69 ask more, 17 less). 5.4 s on PGlite for the whole file.

## The distance the quay quotes, measured again (the same migration)

The owner's order was "do all", and a quote that disagrees with the voyage is a visible fault. `public.sea_reaches.reaches` is the distance the quay quotes between two places. It was the search's distance between the roadsteads as both stood before 0440. Since then:

- 0440 moved 69 roadsteads and opened three straits;
- since 0440 a fleet sails the course author's course (the search, then the re-sailing off the drawn land), which is longer than the search's;
- 0441 changed the search's rule.

So the voyage was no longer the distance quoted. Proof 4 showed it (508.35 nm sailed out of Lisbon, 505 quoted), and 0441 let that proof to 1 %.

**Measured** (`scripts/ops/harness/seas-memo/measure-reaches.mjs`, every one of the 35,778 pairs of the 268 places, one flood a place, 3 minutes):

| the new figure is | pairs that move | median | 95 in 100 under | over 5 % | largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the search's own course | 20,432 | 0.05 % | 0.96 % | 52 | Messina to Zakynthos 291.3 -> 630.4 |
| **the course a fleet really sails** (taken) | 34,461 | 0.14 % | 1.39 % | 251 | Messina to Zakynthos 291.3 -> 629.7 |

**The prices.** `public.port_goods.haul` multiplies a good's price for the sea between a city and its nearest maker (0173). It is derived from these distances by `world.haul_rows()`, and the world guard holds the rows equal to it. So new distances re-price market rows. Measured on the chain's world, inside a rolled-back transaction:

| the new figure is | market rows whose multiplier changes | median price change | 95 in 100 under | 99 in 100 under | rows over 1 % | rows over 2 % | largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the search's own course | 54,620 of 159,766 | 0.018 % | 0.29 % | 1.09 % | 651 | 47 | -2.85 % |
| **the course a fleet really sails** (taken) | 104,296 of 159,766 | 0.047 % | 0.48 % | 1.24 % | 1,806 | 244 | +6.03 % |

That is inside the lead's line (small: under about 1 to 2 % on the affected rows), so it is done here. The 244 rows over 2 % are goods whose nearest maker is across a pair that was badly quoted: the quote was wrong, and the price was derived from the wrong quote.

**What 0442 does for it:**

- every pair's distance is the length of the course a fleet really sails, one figure both ways;
- every market row's haul is refilled from `world.haul_rows()`, as 0208 and 0212 did (137,384 rows change their haul distance; 104,296 of them their multiplier). No knob moves;
- `public.price_history` is not rescaled (decided by default): 0212 rescaled it for a change of hundreds of times; here a chart steps by a fraction of a per cent;
- the proofs sail what a player sails: `scripts/db/proof-courses.mjs` re-sails its courses off the coast mask too, and proof 4 is back at half a per cent.

**One pair to look at.** Messina to Zakynthos is now 630 nm where the old table said 291: after 0440 the course between their roadsteads goes round Sicily, not through the Strait of Messina. The quote is now what is sailed. Whether Messina's roadstead should lie where the strait can be passed is a question for the land work, not for this file.

## So that it cannot happen again

- The templates of both re-sailing generators (`scripts/land-channels.sql.mjs`, `scripts/isthmus.sql.mjs`) call `public.sea_leg_asks_keep()` in their one step, after the courses and the roadsteads are written. Their headers say to re-run the build.
- `tests/sea.noLandCrossing.spec.ts` (the land guard, which runs on any migration that writes courses or roadsteads) reads the three laws on the built world and fails on any row out of step.
- The world guard already holds the table equal to `data/sea-legs.json`, so a migration whose step adds a sea fails `npm run build` until the build is re-run.
- Proof 144 holds the three laws and the function. Proofs 97 and 98 keep their laws.
- The procedure is in `docs/NAVIGATION_PLAN.md` section 8.

## Proof 98's courses

Proof 98 holds 506 plotted courses (from Acapulco and from Sakai to every other harbour) and compares Trade's tag with the door on each. They were the courses plotted for 0403. They are now the courses of this build (`--proof98` rewrites the block). The proof's law is unchanged.

## Self-assert (properties)

- The seed is `data/sea-legs.json` (md5), plotted on this world's harbours, roadsteads, raster and sea boxes (md5 each), or the file refuses.
- One row a pair of harbours.
- A rolled-back probe: a lane's row cut to one sea and its asks set to nothing, a harbour's own row given another sea. The three laws see each; the function mends each.
- After the step: no own row, no lane and no row's asks out of step; Galle's own row is its roadstead's sea; no row lost a sea of its plotted course.

## Not here (listed)

- A player's saved route is not in the table: the door judges its own course.
- `public.price_history` is not rescaled for the new hauls (above).
- **The database.** The refill rewrites about 137,000 rows of `public.port_goods` once on prod (the drift job rewrites about 10,400 a run). Read the gate before the deploy.
