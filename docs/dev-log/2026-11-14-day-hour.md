# 2026-11-14: a game day lasts one hour (0413)

The owner, 2026-11-14: **"change the game day - 1 hour."** He had been told a game day is 12 real minutes, and that a longer day means fewer events per real minute on a database that is too small.

Decided by default (the coordinator, the same day): the day he sees goes from 12 minutes to 60, a factor of 5, and **everything keeps its proportion**: the ships and the market slow together. Every decision below marked **(default)** can be reversed.

## What it does to play, first

A voyage is the same number of days and costs the same. It takes five times as long on the wall clock. The market moves five times slower too, so a price walks as far during a passage as it did before.

| First voyages from Lisbon (a new company's own ship, about 16 knots; her roll moves it a little) | miles | voyage-days | before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Setubal | 38 | 0.10 | 9 s | 45 s |
| Porto | 158 | 0.41 | 36 s | about 3 min |
| Seville | 284 | 0.73 | 66 s | about 5.5 min |

No first voyage is longer than a sitting. A sea delay is longer too: one calm costs 1 to 4 voyage-days, which was 1.5 to 6 minutes and is 7.5 to 30 minutes.

A typical merchant lap, from the 861 merchant loops of the local world (median loop: 4 stops, 3,283 miles, 18.9 knots, 7.1 voyage-days of sailing):

| Merchant lap | sailing days | before (sailing + 2 min rest + a minute a call) | after (sailing + 10 min rest + a minute a call) |
|---|---|---|---|
| a short loop (the quarter mark) | 3.4 | about 11 min | about 40 min |
| the median loop | 7.1 | about 17 min | about 67 min |
| a long loop (the nine-in-ten mark) | 41.6 | about 68 min | about 5 h 26 min |

These are loop lengths from the local world, not prod's laps. `03-laps-and-passages.sql` under `C:/Users/gkwng/dev/day-hour-reads/` reads prod's own.

What a player will notice besides slower ships: a price now changes about every 50 minutes at a harbour (it was about every 10); a request stands five real days (it was one); the date turns once an hour.

## The first finding: there was no one day

"A game day is 12 minutes" was true of one thing only: the clock in the top bar and the Sun on the map. Reading the code found four different days, none derived from another on the server.

| The "day" | Length before | Where it lived | What runs on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The clock's day (top bar hour, the Sun) | 720 s | a **client constant only** (`GAME_CLOCK_RATE = TIME_COMPRESSION / 8`); the server had no such number | the hour shown, day and night on the map |
| The date's day (top bar date) | 2,880 s | a client constant (a month is one real day) | the date shown |
| A voyage-day | 90 s | `world_config.time_compression` 960 (86,400 / 960) | sailing, supplies, wages, weather and raid rolls, every "N days" on a screen |
| The market's day | 60 s on prod (2,880 in the chain; the owner set 60 by hand on 2026-10-30) | `world_config.game_day_seconds` | stock regeneration, daily trade caps, requests' deadlines, a route's laps per day |

So the 12-minute day was eight voyage-days. `game_day_seconds` is the market's day, not the sailing day: a fleet settles one voyage-day every 90 seconds.

## The one authority now

- **The rate.** `world_config.time_compression` stays the one rate: **960 -> 192**. A voyage-day is 450 s. New `clock_voyage_days` (8) states the ratio the client only assumed, so the day's length is derived, never typed: 86,400 x 8 / 192 = **3,600 s**.
- **The door.** `public.clock_set(compression, at)`, owner only, is the one way to move the rate. It moves everything below together, by the same factor.
- **The counters.** `public.clock_count(kind, at)` and its inverse `public.clock_start(kind, count)` are the one authority for the market's four counters: game day, drift slot, want period, population slot. `world.game_day`, `public.drift_slot_of`, `public.want_period` and `public.next_drift_change_at` are now one line each over them. `public.tick_market_drift` and `public.population_tick` no longer divide the epoch themselves; `world.contracts` and `public.city_coupons` no longer multiply a day number by the knob (they were the only two places that did).
- **The client** reads the rate, the ratio and the clock's anchor from the snapshot (`setServedClock` in `src/lib/format/time.ts`). Its own 960 and 8 are only what is drawn before the first snapshot.

Nine hunks over nine bodies, one line each. Three new functions.

## What the door moves, and how each stays continuous

| Thing | Before -> after (prod's values where known) | How it crosses the switch |
|---|---|---|
| Sailing (players, merchants, cargo ships, navy, pirates: all sail through `voyages`) | a voyage-day 90 s -> 450 s | every voyage at sea is re-timed about the instant: `t' = at + (t - at) x 5` on departure and arrival. Same mile, same settled days, same next boundary at the instant |
| The market's day (`game_day_seconds`): regeneration, daily caps, haggles, requests, laps per day, city treasury days | 60 -> 300 s | the count at the instant is what it was; the day it is in is stretched about the instant the same way; a past instant reads as it did |
| The drift slot (`drift_slot_seconds`) | 60 -> 300 s | the same |
| The want period (`want_period_seconds`) | x5 | the same |
| The population's step (`population_slot_seconds`, new: it was the literal 600) | 600 -> 3,000 s | the same. The job still runs every 10 minutes and steps nothing inside a slot already stepped |
| The drift job's spread (`drift_spread_runs`, 0409) | 10 -> 50 runs | a harbour is stepped once in the same number of slots (ten); the job writes a fifth of the rows a minute |
| The press's half-life (`glut_half_life_hours`) | 3 -> 15 h | read when a row is next stepped |
| A city investment's half-life (`invested_half_life_hours`) | 72 -> 360 h | read at the next hourly roll |
| Navy upkeep (`navy_upkeep_day`; the pirates' ships pay it too) | 60,000 -> 12,000 a real day | paid hourly: the next hour's slice is the new one |
| Dividends (`works_coupon_day`) | 9 % -> 1.8 % of what was paid, a real day | see the seam below |
| The population's tax (`population_tax_per_1000`) | 4,000 -> 800 | paid once a real day |
| The pirates' spending cap (`pirate_spend_day_pct`) | 0.5 % -> 0.1 % of the hoard a real day | read live |
| The top bar's date and hour, the Sun | a day 720 s -> 3,600 s | anchored: the date as printed and the hour as it stood at the instant; from then on ONE clock, the date turns at the hour hand's midnight. A month is 30 real hours (was 24) **(default)** |

**The invariant, proven (proof 110, `DH_MARKET_SAME`):** Lisbon's 629 market rows, staged the same, stepped by the real drift job 100 voyage-days on at the old rate (9,000 s) and 100 voyage-days on after the switch (45,000 s), with the walk's noise at 0. Every row's drift, stock, press, slots stepped (10) and days regenerated (3) are the same as text. In the same 9,000 real seconds after the switch, 2 slots pass instead of 10.

## What stays in real time (a person's pacing)

| Thing | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Port time: "every port charges and takes a minute" | 60 s | in game terms a call is now 0.13 voyage-days, was 0.67 |
| A battle, its rounds | 600 s, 60 s | holds a fleet 1.3 voyage-days, was 6.7 |
| Attack cooldown, a ship attacked once in an hour, WANTED | 30 min, 60 min, 48 h | |
| The re-ask back-off, laid-up, refit | 10/20/40/50 min, 6 h, 30 min | a laid-up merchant is asked five times as often per voyage-day |
| Gift cooldown, gifts and refounds per week | 24 h, a real week | |
| Navy lie-up, pirate haunt, navy build, pirate founding | 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, 2 h | patrols turn five times as often per voyage-day **(default)** |
| Skill training, daily caps (reputation, skill points, donations), the loot cap, regard's half-life | per real hour or UTC day | 0317's rule: a real day is never a game day |
| A request's first six hours are the players' (`contract_npc_after_hours`) | 6 h | |
| Stats, history, standings, reconcile, every cron cadence | real time | their load does not shrink |

Two that are counts of market days and so follow the clock **(default; each is one knob to reverse)**:
- **A request's deadline** (`contract_deadline_days` 1,440 market days): it was 24 real hours and is now 120. In voyage-days it is what it was (960). To keep 24 real hours, set it to 288.
- **A route's laps per market day**: one lap per 5 minutes instead of per minute.

## Real-day costs against a house's income

No merchant or player house pays anything per real day: wages, stores and wear are per voyage-day, dock fees per call. The flows per real day are all on the cities' side, and all four were scaled:

| Flow | Who pays whom | Size before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| Navy upkeep | a capital to the harbour its ship last called at | 60,000 a ship a day; 43 navy ships on prod is about 2.6 million a day | 12,000 a ship |
| Dividends | a city to its shareholders, only from its own profit | 9 % a day of what was paid in | 1.8 % |
| Population tax | a harbour's people to its city | about 10.1 million a day over all cities in the local world | about 2.0 million |
| Pirates' spending | the hoard, capped | 0.5 % of the hoard a day | 0.1 % |

I could not size these against a typical house's income: that needs prod (`04-real-day-flows.sql`). What can be said without it: a city's income from trade falls to a fifth per real day, and these four now fall with it, so each is the same per voyage-day.

## The traps, and what was done

**(a) Fleets already at sea.** Re-timed about the instant, as above. Proven on probe voyages in the migration and in proof 110 (a plain voyage, a five-day voyage switched in her third day, a voyage held by a battle).
- `departed_at` of a voyage under way at the switch is no longer her true departure (it is earlier) until she lands.
- A battle's hold running across the switch keeps its end; that battle runs its full length. Ten minutes at most, once.
- A rest or repair already running keeps its stored end.

**(b) The calendar does not jump.** `tests/format.spec.ts` holds that the date, the hour and the Sun are the same at the instant before and after, and that the date then turns exactly when the hour passes midnight.

**(c) Nothing pays or regenerates five at once, nothing stops** (proof 110):
- `DH_DAYS`: voyage days: 2 complete before and after; settling at the switch settles nothing; exactly one settles at the new boundary.
- `DH_MARKET_CONT`: at the switch the drift job steps 0 rows and regenerates 0; a hair before the period's re-timed end, 0; a hair after the slot's end all 629 rows once, by one slot's step (0.1 -> 0.09, not theta to the fifth); a hair after the day's end all 629 regenerate one day.
- `DH_COUNTERS`: each of the four counters reads the same count at the switch and for an instant three days back; its next start is five times as far off; moved back at the same instant it returns to the same next start.
- `DH_REQUEST`: an open request keeps its day number. Its end is served five times as far off; it is still open a second after its old end and a second before its new one, and expires a second after.
- `DH_DIVIDEND`: a share stamped six hours before the switch was due 210,000 before it and is due 42,000 at the first coupon after it: a fifth, not five times and not nothing.

**(d) The seams that remain.**
- The first dividend after the switch is paid at the new rate for its whole real day, though part of that day ran at the old rate: a city pays up to four fifths of one day's coupon less, once.
- The Stats' "an hour ago", "a day ago" and "a week ago" lookups count slots. For the first week they read across the seam at the new slot length, so those three comparisons are off until the old rows age out. No money hangs on them.
- After a rollback, an instant before 0413's own switch is read at the length of the time between the two moves (the anchor keeps one earlier length). Only reads of the past by day number see it.

**(e) The economy's balance.** Per voyage-day: wages, supplies, wear, raid rolls, dock fees, regeneration, drift, wants, population steps, navy upkeep, dividends and the population's tax are all what they were. What is **not** the same per voyage-day is the list under "What stays in real time".

**(f) A new player's first hour.** The table at the top.

## Measured in the local world

`scripts/ops/harness/day-hour/measure.mjs --n=150 --warm=4 --mins=8`: one local world, the proofs' market, 150 merchants lit and at sea, the clock played by hand as pg_cron plays it. Eight minutes at 960; then the migration is applied to that running world (130 voyages at sea were re-timed by it); then eight more minutes. This run was made before the market's clock was added to the file; the sailing figures do not depend on it.

| A real minute | before (960) | after (192) | after / before |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrivals settled | 37.6 | 5.8 | 0.15 |
| Voyage days settled | 100.5 | 19.4 | 0.19 |
| Lap starts | 12.4 | 0.0 | see below |
| The beat, mean | 18 ms | 3 ms | 0.19 |
| Lap-start planning, a minute | 1,477 ms | 11 ms | 0.01 |
| Harbours the drift job steps (of 263 here) | 26 | 6 | 0.2 |
| Market rows the drift job rewrites (of 156,621) | about 15,700 | about 3,100 | 0.2 |

Lap starts read 0 because the window is the eight minutes right after the switch: every lap under way was stretched five times, so none ended in it. In the steady state lap starts follow arrivals. The drift job's figures are counted from `public.drift_ports_due`, not timed.

What does **not** shrink: the beat's fixed cost each second; stats, history, standings, reconcile; the waiting-asks job (the back-off is in real minutes). The client's own re-read of the world goes from every 7.5 s to every 30 s.

## Found on the way (not this slice's to fix)

- **A battle that ends early can cost the held fleet one extra voyage-day** (live today, at 960 too). `voyages.delay_hours` keeps three decimal places; a cut hold's own hours are kept to ten (`voyage.hold_cut`). When the row's figure rounds up, `voyage.total_days` counts one more day: one more day of stores, wages and a weather roll at arrival. Proof 60 cut at 200 s, which shows it at 192; it now cuts at 225 s, whose hours are whole at both rates. `public.clock_set` keeps a re-timed hold's hours to three places so that it cannot cause this itself.
- **Proof 61 (the yard plans the build) is red on the merged heads without this file**: `YARD_PLAN_BUILD` fails after 0409 + 0410 + 0411. Not the clock.
- Running the proofs re-keyed the shared prefix cache to this branch's chain. Another worktree's next run re-applies its last few files once.

## Known risks

- **Browser specs that sail a real voyage take five times as long.** `tests/arrivalWait.fixture.ts` reads the rate from the authority; specs with their own fixed waits may go red on main's Acceptance run.
- Between the migration's deploy and the client's publish (minutes), an open browser still assumes 960 until it reloads.
- Prod's `want_period_seconds` and the other hand-set values were not read: the door multiplies whatever stands there.
