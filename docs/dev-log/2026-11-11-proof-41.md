# 2026-11-11 — proof 41's red on main: a rounded leg, and a world that is not built the same twice

## The red

`pglite-gate (the rest)`, `scripts/db/proofs/41_wanted.sql`: `PROOF 41 FAIL: the merchant did not settle
every day`, 6/20 markers. Red on main's runs 42972085809 (0348) and 42975176695 (0349) and on every
migration PR since (#585). Last green: main's 0347 run 42959903571.

## The cause, in the proof

The proof's `pg_temp.legs` made each leg `round(voyage.fleet_speed(fleet) * 24, 2)` nm: "one voyage day
to a leg". For a house that rounding does nothing (a new house has no captain and no skill, so her speed
x 24 is her hull's two-decimal speed x 18). For a merchant it does: the first docked merchant by
`roster_ord`, Carreira da Índia, sails at 14.4245 kn full (nau 13.20 + 2.70 of fittings = 15.90, x 0.75
full, x 1.08 her navigator, x 1.12 Navigation 3). 24 hours are 346.188 nm; the leg was 346.19. Six legs
are 144.0008 hours, `voyage.total_days` is `ceil(hours / 24)` = 7, `voyage.settle` writes a seventh day
(correct), and the check wants exactly 6 events.

In the local world 95 of 837 merchants have a speed that rounds up like this (153 round down, 589 are
exact). Over every four-decimal speed from 0.0001 to 40 kn the old leg gives a seventh day for 192,005
of 400,000.

## The fix (proofs only; no migration, no `voyage.settle`)

A leg is her speed x 24 **to the digit**, not rounded. `nm / speed` is then exactly 24 (an exact numeric
division: 0 of 400,000 speeds, and 0 of the 837 merchants, answer anything else on PGlite's PostgreSQL
18.3), n legs are n days exactly, and the check stays `= cardinality(v_seas)`, not a range.

Why not round down, or stop short of the day: proof 38 asserts day d is in leg d
(`voyage.leg_at_day(v, d).sea_id = sea_at(d)`), which holds only when the day's end is not past the
leg's end. A leg a hair short of a day puts every day's end in the next leg. Exact legs keep both: day d
ends on leg d's end, and the voyage is n days.

The same `round(speed * 24, 2)` was copied (not shared) in two other proofs; all five places are changed
the same way:

- `scripts/db/proofs/41_wanted.sql` `pg_temp.legs`
- `scripts/db/proofs/38_sea_danger.sql` `pg_temp.fifty_one`, and the 12-day and 8-day single legs
- `scripts/db/proofs/46_pirate_ships_sail.sql` `pg_temp.through`

## Why two builds of the same chain disagree: the world build is not deterministic

A merchant hull's fittings are chosen by her **fleet's id**: `public.npc_fit_loadout` (0126) takes
`md5(fleet_id) mod` the class's pattern count, and `fleets.id` defaults to `gen_random_uuid()`, drawn
when the chain is applied. So every from-zero build gives each merchant fleet a fresh pattern. A nau has
three patterns (`npc_loadouts`), and for Carreira da Índia they give:

| pattern | fittings' speed | fleet speed | 24 h | old leg | old verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | +2.55 | 14.2884 | 342.9216 | 342.92 (down) | 6 days, green |
| 1 | +3.60 | 15.2410 | 365.7840 | 365.78 (exact) | 6 days, green |
| 2 | +2.70 | 14.4245 | 346.1880 | 346.19 (up) | 7 days, red |

Her other inputs are authored and the same in every build: three nau at roll 1.000 (0276 backfills
nothing), hull whole, the proof fills her hold, her navigator Bartolomeu Dias at 8 % in an exploration
cabin (a merchant's captain is never homesick), Navigation 3 x 4 %. The local world drew pattern 2. The
world CI kept until 0347 must have drawn 0 or 1; the world main's 0348 run built from zero drew 2. One
from-zero build in three makes this proof red.

Not fixed here: 0126 is an applied migration and frozen. Known siblings of the same kind, not touched:
0276's ship roll is seeded by the ship's id (also `gen_random_uuid()`), 0010's market drift is
`random()` (the market fixture exists for that). A proof that reads a merchant's own figures should not
assume them.

## Why main's 0348 run applied from zero

Run 42972085809, job `pglite-gate (the rest)`: `Cache not found for input keys:
chain-prefix-01725a01…, chain-prefix-` and then `prefix cache: no cached prefix for this chain; applying
all 332` (3855.9 s). The restore key `chain-prefix-` matches any entry main may read, so main had none
left. Main's 0347 run had saved `chain-prefix-bc360421…` at 04:43:24Z, 1 h 41 min earlier; it is no
longer in the repository's cache list. The repository's caches stand at 10.73 GB in 31 entries, over
GitHub's 10 GB limit, with each chain-prefix, world-image and growth-house entry 0.66 to 0.71 GB; five
of that size still listed were saved between 04:43 and 06:24 (PR #576's two chain-prefix and two
world-image entries, main's growth-house). GitHub evicts the least recently used entries over the limit, and main's
chain-prefix entry went. PR #576's entry under the very same key existed at that minute, but a pull
request's cache is not readable from main.

Was it expected: allowed, not intended. On a push to main the pglite legs run without
`BYEHARU_PREFIX_CACHE=required` (main is never refused), so the run applied from zero and said so. The
`disposable-chain` job of the same run restored its snapshot (2.5 minutes). The eviction is inferred
from the limit and the missing entry; GitHub keeps no eviction log.

- The eviction itself (above).
