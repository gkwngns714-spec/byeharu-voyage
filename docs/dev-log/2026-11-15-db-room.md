## Why it grows

**a. 0412's fixed thresholds are now bigger than the tables.** 0412 (11-14 morning) set the churn tables to scale factor 0 with
50,000 to 200,000 dead rows, sized for tables five to ten times today's (events then 555 MB). 0409's retention then cut the live rows.
A dead row's slot is reused only after a vacuum pass, so each table grows to its live rows **plus** its threshold before its first pass:

About 470,000 dead rows held on purpose. More ships at sea since 0423 / 0425 fill the quotas faster (voyages ~370,000 dead rows a day,
orders ~640,000), so the tables reach their ceiling sooner and the database creeps up between passes.

**b. The stats and price tiers are still filling.** 0421 doubled the Stats count (4 an hour) and the fine tier keeps 72 hours; the hourly
tiers keep 30 days and the world is ~23 days old (the day fold marks stand at 10-16). stats_good went 79 -> 103 MB since 20:30;
price_history_hour is 155 MB at 703,000 rows and reaches ~842,000 at day 30. At the rules of today that is about +100 MB more.

**Not the cause.** No long transaction: the oldest open one was 1 s, every backend's xmin age under 10. Not port_goods: it has stood at
92-93 MB in every read since 11-14 18:01 and has not been vacuumed since 11-01; 2,886,769,321 of its 2,922,143,841 updates are HOT and are
pruned on the page. Since 0420 reprices every harbour every 15 minutes (~12,300 updates a minute), its dead share is a saw from 2 % to 35 %
every 13 minutes (2 % and 22.6 % in two reads 4 minutes apart). `daily.sql` now leaves it out of the `dead_pct` line (its size still shows).

**A hidden cost found on the way (not changed):** the price-snapshot job's hour prune (`delete ... where hour < ...`) has no index on
`hour`, so it reads the whole of price_history_hour every 10 minutes. At 8 days of hours that is ~28 MB instead of 87.

## What 0428 does

1. **The sweeper scales with the table** (budget rule 5): 13 tables of 0412 (city_wants untouched) at
   `autovacuum_vacuum_scale_factor` 0.1 and `autovacuum_vacuum_threshold` 5,000; price_history_hour 0.05 and 5,000 (was 1,000 with
   scale factor 0: the daily read's `autovac_low` line). 0412's pace (cost delay 20 ms, cost limit 25) on all fourteen; insert and analyze
   settings kept. A table now carries at most ~15 % dead. ~330 passes a day in all (the migration header has the table), over 2-60 MB
   tables that sit in the cache: about 500,000 page visits a day, against 44 million a day before 0412.
2. **Stats: 30 hours of fine points, a week of hours.** `stats_fine_hours` 72 -> 30, `stats_hour_days` 30 -> 8.
   Who reads what, and why it still reads: a 1-day graph reads 24 hours of fine rows; the day-ago lookups the row at 24 hours; the
   history job closes yesterday at 00:12 from fine rows at most 24 h 12 min old; a good's "7 days ago" price is the row at 7 days (hours
   to 8 days); the 1-week graphs group 7 days by the hour. Past 8 days a row folds into its day (flows added, state kept), as 0246 always
   folded past day 30. The fold is bounded (4 hours and 1 day a run per table): ~22 runs, ~5.5 hours, ~15,000 rows a run at most.
3. **Prices: a week of hours.** `price_history_hour_days` 30 -> 8; the days are kept for ever as before. The first price-snapshot run
   after the deploy prunes ~480,000 hours once (~30 s, ~110 MB of WAL, one time); then ~1,200 rows an hour as now.
4. **A month chart draws a point a day.** `public.stats_span_unit('1m')` -> 'day' (world.stats_overview / stats_city / stats_merchants
   group a month by day) and `world.price_series` '1m' -> price_history_day, 30 days. 1d, 1w, 1y and all are unchanged. A month had 720
   hourly points; it has 30 daily ones (each with its day's low and high for prices), the newest the last whole day, as 1y. The client's
   own copy of the span table (`src/features/stats/statsFaces.ts` `SPAN_PERIOD`) moves with it; three specs follow
   (`rpc.priceSeries`, `rpc.stats`, `wholePeriods`). This is the change 0409 and 0416 named and left; it is what lets the hours go.
5. **City goods' days kept 90 days** (`stats_city_good_keep_days` 30 -> 90): the owner asked for longer in 0416's thread; one row a
   pair a day, ~0.3 MB a day, +18 MB over the next two months.

Decided by default (the owner may reverse): the month graph from days (4); 90 days for the city goods (5); port_goods' fillfactor
left to him (runbook C, optional, -35 MB).

## The space: the runbook

0428 shrinks no file: freed space is reused inside each table. `scripts/ops/harness/db-room/RUNBOOK.md` gives it back with `VACUUM FULL`,
one statement a call, about 6 hours after the deploy (once the folds and the prune have drained):

| step | tables | frees | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (no pause) | price_history_hour 155 -> ~48, stats_good 103 -> ~42, stats_city 76 -> ~35, stats_city_good 65 -> ~47, price_history 24 -> ~14 | ~237 | ~765 |
| B (clock stopped, 1-5 min) | voyages 84 -> ~40, events 49 -> ~21, orders 37 -> ~12, trade_daily 27 -> ~3, treasury_flows 21 -> ~3, voyage_events 46 -> ~34, insured_voyages 14 -> ~3, standing_route_laps 11 -> ~4, ledger 10 -> ~4 | ~175 | **~590** |
| C (optional) | port_goods at fillfactor 50: 93 -> ~58 | ~35 | ~555 |

Then it settles at **~650-680 MB**: each churn table up to ~15 % dead (~20 MB), 0427's battles (~100 rows a minute kept 48 h, ~50 MB),
day rows ~1 MB a day. Hot set ~450 MB. Without the runbook the database stays near 1,000 MB but stops growing.

"after" sizes are estimates: each table's live rows times its row width read from today's heap (heap MB / (live + dead)), its indexes in
proportion, nothing for bloat. They are good to about ±20 %.
