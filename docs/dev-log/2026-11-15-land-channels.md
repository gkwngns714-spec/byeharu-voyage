# The straits are open and the roads are water (2026-11-15, migration 0441, owner row 352)

The owner: *"some ships go pass through land - such as hadibu ... fix this properly"*. 0440 re-sailed every course off the land the chart draws and listed what it could not reach. This slice takes that list to zero.

## What 0440 left, and why

Measured with `scripts/db/audit-land-crossings.mjs` on the 0440 world (now also reading `public.npc_coast_courses`, which 0440 missed):

| on the 0440 world | over 2 nm of drawn land |
|---|---|
| lanes (`npc_courses`) | 52 of 1,141 |
| route legs (`standing_route_stops`) | 50 distinct, 145 rows of 3,350 |
| coast courses (`npc_coast_courses`, 0400) | 2 of 12 (Male, Ambon to Dili: 21.5 nm over Wetar) |
| roadsteads on drawn land | 57 of 268 |
| harbour-to-harbour courses the browser proposes | 1,956 of 71,556 |

Three causes, not one:

1. **A turn on an island.** A course turns at raster cell centres, and the raster cannot see the island, so a turn can sit on drawn land (Strangford Lough on the North Channel, Oland, Saaremaa). The refiner searched segment by segment, so each half started or ended in a pocket of the coast that the 1.5 nm grid cannot leave, and it kept the old course. This was the Hebrides, the North Channel, Moon Sound and Oland, not the raster.
2. **Water the raster closes and the chart draws.** Corfu's North and South channels (1:110m joins the island to Epirus), the northern half of the Tsugaru strait (1:110m draws Hokkaido across it), and the Wielingen, the Western Scheldt's mouth.
3. **A roadstead on the land or in a pocket.** 57 roadsteads on drawn land (Hadibu's quay on Socotra's shore, Corfu's, Jeju's, Stockholm's in the archipelago's islands). A further 8 lie in drawn water that the open sea cannot reach on the 1.5 nm grid (Toulon's inner harbour, New York's upper bay), so every course from them was kept as it was.

Stockholm's own water (Saltsjon) is not joined to the sea even in the 1:10m drawing at 0.75 nm, so no raster cell opened there would help; its roads move into the archipelago instead.

## The fix

- **`src/lib/sea/refine.ts`** (the browser's course author and the generator share it):
  - land runs are measured along the whole course, so one search window spans a turn on an island;
  - a stretch kept from the old course, cut at a window's edge, that touches a raster land cell is searched again (`mend`), by the raster's own search when it is ocean-long;
  - a course along an edge of the land bitmap's pixels is read on both sides of it (Chios).
- **Charted channels** (`scripts/sea-grid.mjs`, `charted: true`). `corfu`, `tsugaru` and `western-scheldt` open 6 raster cells. Unlike a river carve, the land the chart draws in their cells stays land, in the coast mask and in the audit (`riverCarvedCells()`). Each new polyline runs down drawn water; the generator refuses one that touches land. The Corfu control (the town's roads to the channel's north mouth, every sample checked) is refused before and is water after.
- **Eight older straits charted too**: Kerch, Bab-el-Mandeb, Hormuz, Malacca, Sunda, Seto, the White Sea's Gorlo and the Baltic gulfs. The audit had excused all land in a carved cell. Measured: 325 stored courses crossed drawn land inside carved cells, up to 42.7 nm in Malacca. For these straits, the drawn land now counts and 98 more stored courses are re-sailed through them. Measured and left as carves:
  - the Danish straits and the Gulf of Suez. Their drawn water is not continuous on the 1.5 nm grid inside a one-cell carve, and charting them left 65 courses that could not be re-sailed.
  - the Dardanelles and the Bosphorus, which are narrower than the chart's pixel.
  - every river.
- **Roads on the water** (decided by default). 69 harbours are moved to the nearest point to the quay that is drawn water, has 1.5 nm of drawn water round it, and joins the open sea. These are the 57 on land, 8 in pockets, and Riga, Tallinn, Osaka and Sakai, whose roads lay on the land of a now-charted strait.
  - Hormuz and Bandar Abbas shared one raster roadstead, so they share one drawn point.
  - No move is over 12 nm (Stockholm is the most, 11.75 nm), so a stored player route still begins within `course_join_nm` (15 nm).
  - `voyage.drawn_roadsteads` holds each point by its quay, and `voyage.water_roadstead` answers it for that quay (one hunk), so the rule and `public.sea_reaches` stay one answer.
  - `scripts/build-sea-migration.mjs`'s `roadsteadOf` and its emitted `water_roadstead` read the same table, so a later sea migration keeps the points.
  - `reaches` distances are not re-measured (decided by default: a re-measure re-prices the world for a few miles).
- **Migration 0441** (generated by `scripts/build-land-channels.mjs`):
  - opens the 6 cells and names their seas in `sea_cells`;
  - adds 3 rows to `voyage.channels`;
  - creates `voyage.drawn_roadsteads` (static, 69 rows);
  - adds two hunks, each applied once and each reversing:
    - `voyage.water_roadstead`;
    - `public.npc_coast_legs`, which read a coast course backwards as two points and now reads it whole;
  - moves 69 `sea_reaches` roadsteads;
  - re-sails 570 distinct stored courses by `md5(course::text)`, as 0440 did: 569 lanes, 1,673 route stops, 3 coast courses, and the frozen pieces of cargo circuits and (left out by 0440) navy patrols.

  Self-assert:
  - the cells are water;
  - the Corfu control is refused before and is water after;
  - every moved roadstead is what the rule and the table answer, Hadibu's included;
  - every new course passes `voyage.path_refusal`;
  - a rolled-back probe re-cuts a cargo leg and a navy patrol leg to the new pieces, and a multi-point coast course is read backwards whole.

  It applies on the world image in about 20 s. The forward rollback (`scripts/ops/harness/land-channels/rollback-0441.sql`) was run on the image after 0441: the raster, seas, reaches, both function bodies, lanes, coast courses, route stops and channels came back byte-identical.

## Not fixed here (listed, not hidden)

- **Panama City and Portobelo.** Their roadsteads are about 46 nm apart across the isthmus. The 25 nm approach allowance at each end (`voyage.path_refusal`, and the search's own 21 nm) lets a straight line between them pass unchecked, which works as a canal. This is the server's approach rule, not a channel or a roadstead, and it needs its own slice. 0091 already recorded the same allowance on Panama City to Port Royal.
- **River carves.** The chart draws land over water the game sails on purpose (the Thames, the Gironde, the Elbe, the Chao Phraya), and in the Danish straits, the Gulf of Suez and the Turkish straits the fine grid cannot pass. The audit excuses those cells by design.
- **Overlap with #511** ("islands on the chart", OWNER HOLD, not merged and not reused here). #511 adds 28 island polygons to the 1:110m land file and moves their roadsteads. This slice moves the roadsteads of several of the same harbours (Hagatna, Funchal, Male, Naha, Jeju, Tsushima, Valletta and others) by the drawn-roadstead rule instead. If #511 is ever taken up, it must be re-cut on top of 0441: its raster closes cells, so `build-land-channels.mjs` and its audit must run again.
