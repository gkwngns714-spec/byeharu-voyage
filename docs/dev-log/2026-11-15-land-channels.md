## Why some courses were left over, and the fix

1. **A turn on an island.** A course turns at raster cell centres, and the raster cannot see the island, so a turn can sit on drawn land: Strangford Lough on the North Channel, Oland, Saaremaa. The refiner searched segment by segment, so each half started or ended in a pocket of the coast that the 1.5 nm grid cannot leave. In `src/lib/sea/refine.ts` (shared by the browser and the generator):
   - land is now measured along the whole course, so one search window spans the turn;
   - a stretch kept from the old course whose samples touch a raster land cell is searched again (`mend`; an ocean-long stretch by the raster's own search);
   - a course on an edge of the land bitmap's pixels is read on both sides of it.
2. **Water the raster closes and the chart draws.** `charted: true` channels in `scripts/sea-grid.mjs`:
   - `corfu` (both channels), `tsugaru` (the strait's northern half, which 1:110m draws as Hokkaido) and `western-scheldt` (the Wielingen) open 6 raster cells;
   - eight older straits are charted too: Kerch, Bab-el-Mandeb, Hormuz, Malacca, Sunda, Seto, the White Sea and the Baltic gulfs. The audit had excused all land in a carved cell, and 325 stored courses crossed it there, up to 42.7 nm in Malacca;
   - a charted channel's drawn land stays land in the coast mask and in the audit; only a river carve's cells are excused (`riverCarvedCells()`);
   - the Danish straits and the Gulf of Suez were measured and left as carves: their drawn water is not continuous on the 1.5 nm grid inside a one-cell carve, and charting them left 65 courses that could not be re-sailed;
   - the Corfu control (the town's roads up the channel, every sample checked) is refused before and is water after.
3. **A roadstead on the land, or in a pocket.** 69 harbours are moved to the nearest point to the quay that is drawn water, has 1.5 nm of drawn water round it, and joins the open sea (decided by default). That is 59 on land and 10 in pockets.
   - Hadibu is now 3.4 nm off Socotra's north shore.
   - Hormuz and Bandar Abbas share one point.
   - Stockholm moves the most, 11.75 nm: its own water (Saltsjon) is not joined to the sea even in the 1:10m drawing.
   - Every move is under 12 nm, so a stored route's first point stays inside `course_join_nm` (15 nm).
   - `voyage.drawn_roadsteads` holds the points, and `voyage.water_roadstead` answers them (one hunk).
   - `public.sea_reaches` matches, and `scripts/build-sea-migration.mjs` reads the same table.
   - `reaches` distances are not re-measured (decided by default).

## The migration (generated by `scripts/build-land-channels.mjs <base image> --prod prod-courses.json`)

- **The fix list** covers every distinct course of the chain's world AND of production's export: 1,680 courses, of which 1,166 crossed. 1,323 are re-sailed, 0 still cross, and they are 0.30% longer on average.
  - Each is named by `md5(course::text)`.
  - One line of points is ONE new course, whatever its text: prod writes the same lane as `51.45` and as `51.4500`. Its reverse takes that course backwards.
  - Every replaced course is also entered read backwards, unless that text is already stored. A row the world writes after the export is a copy of a stored course: a rehomed merchant sails a lane backwards (`public.npc_rehome`), and a loop takes the legs it knows (`public.npc_feasible_legs`). Such a row is caught.
- **On prod at the export** it rewrites 1,070 of 1,351 lanes, 1,970 of 2,535 route stops and 3 coast courses. It re-cuts every frozen cargo and patrol leg built from them.
- **The leg re-cut is new.** Each frozen leg is walked once, left to right, and the longest replaced lane whose pieces the leg holds at that point (matched by their ends) is swapped for its new pieces. 0440's draft replaced piece TEXT. On prod's lanes a short lane's piece lies inside a longer lane's, so the replacements cut each other. The draft's own self-assert caught this on the prod-shaped world.
- **Two hunks**, each applied once and each reversing:
  - `voyage.water_roadstead`;
  - `public.npc_coast_legs`, which read a coast course backwards as two points.
- **Self-assert, on properties only, never on totals that drift.**
  - the charted cells are water;
  - the Corfu control is refused before and is water after;
  - each moved roadstead is what the rule and the table answer;
  - the Hadibu to Kilwa control is off Socotra;
  - every new course passes `voyage.path_refusal`;
  - a rolled-back probe re-cuts a cargo leg and a navy patrol leg, and a multi-point coast course is read backwards whole.

  It does not fail on rows the live world writes while it runs.
- **Voyages already sailing** finish their course.

## So that it cannot happen again

- **The server draws no new geometry.** Every function that writes a course was read on the chain.
  - Lanes and coast courses are seeded only by migrations.
  - A merchant's loop (`npc_found`, `npc_plan`, `npc_rehome`) is a lane, a coast course or another merchant loop, forwards or backwards.
  - A cargo circuit's or a navy patrol's leg is pieces of lanes.
  - So once the stored courses are clean, everything the server writes from them is clean.
- **New geometry comes only from the browser**: a player's route (`cmd.standing_route_save_for`) or a plotted voyage (`cmd.do_sail`). The course author re-sails every course off the coast mask before it offers it. A player's route never becomes a merchant's: `npc_known_legs` reads NPC routes only.
- **The guard** `tests/sea.noLandCrossing.spec.ts` runs on every PR that touches the pathfinder, the coast, the ports, or a migration that writes courses. The known list is empty and may only shrink.
- **The daily prod check (the lead).**
  1. Run `npx --yes supabase@2.120.0 db query --linked --output-format json -f scripts/ops/harness/no-land/prod-courses.sql` (and `prod-legs.sql`), read-only, two statements.
  2. Then run `node scripts/db/audit-land-crossings.mjs <image of prod's chain> --prod courses.json --legs legs.json`.
  3. It fails on anything not on `scripts/db/land-crossings.prod-known.json`, which is empty and may only shrink.

  No GitHub workflow holds prod's keys, so a nightly job would need the owner to add a secret.
- **What a server-side guard would take (not done here).** The browser re-sails, but nothing on the server stops a stale browser tab (a player who has not reloaded since the deploy) or a hand-made call from saving a course over drawn land.
  - SQL can hold the coast mask: about 9,630 cells, 50 bytes each, about 0.5 MB.
  - It could sample a submitted course inside the listed cells and refuse runs over 2 nm at `cmd.standing_route_save_for` and `cmd.do_sail`.
  - It is left for its own slice because it would change live play:
    - every merchant departure passes `cmd.do_sail` again (0120), so it would cost every departure on the smallest instance;
    - it would refuse orders the server accepts today.
  - The daily check catches what it would stop.

## Not fixed here (listed)

- **Panama City and Portobelo.** Their roadsteads are about 46 nm apart across the isthmus. The 25 nm approach allowance at each end (`voyage.path_refusal`; the search's own 21 nm) lets a straight line between them pass unchecked: a canal. This is the server's approach rule, its own slice (0091 recorded the same allowance on Panama City to Port Royal).
- **River carves** (the Thames, the Gironde, the Elbe and others) and the Danish, Suez and Turkish straits: the audit excuses those cells by design.
- **Overlap with #511** ("islands on the chart", OWNER HOLD; not merged, not reused). #511 moves the roadsteads of 28 island harbours by adding island polygons. 0440 moves several of the same ones (Hagatna, Ponta Delgada, Funchal, Male, Naha, Jeju, Tsushima, Valletta) by the drawn-roadstead rule. If #511 is taken up, re-cut it on top of 0440 and run `build-land-channels.mjs` again.
