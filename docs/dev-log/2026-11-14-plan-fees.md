# 2026-11-14: a merchant counts the port's fee before she sails (0406)

**State: built, PR open, not deployed.** Prod head 0404; 0405 (#661, the waiting line's job) lands first. 0406 touches none of 0405's objects and
applies on 0404 or on 0405. It changes live play (which loops merchants choose, who waits), so it gets an after-check.

## The finding

About 25 merchant houses lie laid up with an empty purse (`docs/dev-log/2026-11-14-layup-line.md`). A read-only prod query on them (2026-11-14):

- 13 are pirate houses: the capital's gift pass leaves the pirates out by rule (0341). Not this slice.
- 11 are ordinary houses. Ten of them were gifted their capital three times in a week (the pass's limit) and emptied again, each day earning
  about what it lost.
- The one ledger still kept (Hwang Dohoe, 11-13; a merchant's ledger is kept six hours): sold 31,127 in 17 sales, bought 25,759 in 16, so the
  trade made 5,368. PORT_FEE 8,570 in 44 calls, tax 754, wages about 85. **The dock fees ate more than the trade made.**

## The cause, in the code as it stands at 0404

- What a lap of a loop leaves a merchant is priced in one place, `public.npc_plan`:
  `v_net := v_profit - public.wages_from_profit(v_wages, v_profit) - public.stores_cost(...)`
  (`supabase/migrations/20260818000142_a_merchant_sells_at_the_quays_price_when_she_must.sql:253`). It is the only `v_net := v_profit` in the chain.
  The score, the choice between loops, "a loop pays" (`20260818000197_a_merchants_route_always_pays.sql:220`) and the lay-up (0181) all read it.
- 0237 (`20260818000237_every_port_charges_and_takes_a_minute.sql`) made every harbour a fleet makes charge a dock fee, merchants too, live since
  the economy closed. The fee is `public.port_fee(port, company, cargo at cost)` =
  round((`port_fee_base` + `port_fee_pct` x cargo at cost) x the country factor of her stance there x regard). It is charged by `public.port_call`
  at every arrival: **a call, not a hull; nothing is waived at home** (home is the factor 0.25); a pirate home charges nothing; a crown's ship
  docks free. Prod's knobs: base 250, share 0.005; factors home 0.25, ally 0.5, peace 1, rival 2, war 2. So 62 d. a call at home, 250 at peace, 500
  at a rival's, plus half a per cent of the cargo.
- **Verified: no hunk since 0237 takes the fee from the planner's net.** The live bodies of `public.npc_plan` and `public.npc_leg_lines` at head
  0404 (dumped from the cached world, `scripts/ops/harness/plan-fees/dump.mjs`) contain no `fee` at all.
- A lap's book does not count it either: `cmd.standing_route_close_lap` has no fee in it, so `standing_route_laps.net` and the Stats' merchant
  profit are before dock fees. That is why the laps looked healthy while the purses fell.

So a loop whose margin is under the fees it pays still "pays", is chosen, sailed, written down as a profit, and empties her purse. A poor house is
hit hardest: 0129 sizes every parcel by her purse, and the 250 d. a call does not shrink with it.

## What 0406 does

Eight hunks over two bodies, each asserted to occur once and to reverse; nothing else.

1. `public.npc_leg_lines` also answers `cost`: what the kept parcels of the leg cost her at the source, the sum of the same buy quotes its profit is
   made of (3 hunks). No figure it answered before moves.
2. `public.npc_plan` takes from every loop's net the dock fee of each of its calls, asked of THE one fee: `public.port_fee(the harbour she makes,
   her company, the leg's cost)` (5 hunks). It is the function `public.port_call` charges her by, with the same arguments (there the cargo is
   `public.fleet_stock_at_cost` of her fleet; on arriving that is what she bought at the stop before). No second formula. A leg's fee is asked once
   and kept in the plan's memo with the leg.

Everything that steers by the net follows with no other change: the score, the choice, "pays", the review of a loop that leaves her nothing, and
the lay-up. While the economy is open or the purses dark the fee is 0 and every plan is what it was.

**Expected on prod, honestly.** The effect is at the thin end: houses with a purse of a few thousand (q1 of the lay-up read: 41 sailing fleets under
20,000 d., 10 under 5,000, and the ten gifted-and-emptied houses). Those stop taking loops under their fees: some switch to a loop with fewer or
home calls, some wait in port. So: a few more laid-up at first (my estimate: 10 to 40 fleets, of 885), laps an hour down by about their share

## What a lap's book can and cannot say

`standing_route_laps` keeps sold, bought, supplies, repairs, wages, taxes, insurance and net; **not the dock fee**. So "the share of closed laps
with net after fees under 0" cannot be read from the laps. The checks read it a house at a time: her closed laps' net in the last hour against the
PORT_FEE rows of her ledger in the same hour (whole, since a merchant's ledger is kept six hours).

## Decided by default

1. The fee is counted in the loop's net only. The leg planner's own keep test (a parcel pays 5 % on its outlay) stays before fees.
2. A lap's book is not changed here. Putting the dock fee into `standing_route_laps` (so Stats, the losing count and the lay-up read the truth) is a
   slice of its own.
3. No news row: nothing a player reads changes.
