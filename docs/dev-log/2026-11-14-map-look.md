# The map reads as one picture again (2026-11-14)

The owner, on a phone: *"and fix the graphics overall, especially map. it is weird"* (and row 318 before it: *"the map ui graphic became shit ... especially on phone"*). He did not say what was weird, so the map was looked at, not asked about.

In two days the chart had gained battle rings, news dots, the closed seas' shade and its key, merchants and three more kinds of ship, armadas, the route line with its bar, harbour labels, the fleets corner and three trays. Each was tested alone. Nobody had judged them together.

## How it was looked at

The map was photographed at 390 × 844 and 320 × 640 (touch, 2 device pixels a pixel) and 1,280 × 800, in English and Korean, furthest out, at a sea and at a harbour, over Europe, the Caribbean, East Asia and the open Atlantic, in the shipped world and in the staged ones (merchants on, seas lit, a veteran with eight fleets, an armada's battle, a fight), with the fleets list open, a route planned and a tray open. Every picture was opened and judged.

Before and after, same names: `C:\Users\gkwng\dev\map-look-shots\pairs\before` and `\after`.

## What changed (each at its cause)

1. **One density scale** (`src/chart/glyphs.ts` `wideScale`). A mark that is a picture is full size while a phone holds a coast (20 degrees or less) and shrinks evenly on the zoom's own scale to 0.4 at 0.92 degrees a pixel. It reads degrees a pixel, not the span, so a wide glass showing the same water keeps bigger marks. Three readers and no other: the merchants' hulls and lamps (`FleetsLayer.tsx`), the harbour lights (`harbourLightHalo`, `harbourLightGlow`), the shallows (`CoastlineLayer.tsx`). Not scaled, on purpose: a fleet of yours, a harbour's dot and triangle, a battle's ring, every name.
2. **Merchants are thinned by the glass** (`src/chart/shipMarks.ts` `THIN_DENSITY`): all of them on a coast; three in eight from 0.103 degrees a pixel (a phone holding 40 degrees, a desktop the world); one in eight from 0.33; none from 0.66. By the stable slot of the ship's own id, so the same ships are kept at every pan and none blinks. Your fleets, cargo ships, navy ships and pirate ships are never in the rule; battle rings are not ships and are untouched. The tap reads the same thinning, so a hull that is not drawn cannot be pressed.
3. **A portrait glass is covered** (`src/chart/chartView.ts` `maxSpanX`): furthest out, the view's height is the world's, pole to pole, and east and west are a pan (the sheet has no edge there); the minimap shows the whole. A landscape glass still reaches the whole world. `clampView` and `zoomAt` both read it.
4. **The graticule ends where the world does** (`src/chart/SeaLayer.tsx`).
5. **Round halos** on every name and on the route's words (`LabelsLayer.tsx`, `ShipPathLayer.tsx`).
6. **The shade** (`src/chart/useClosedSeasVeil.ts`): a dusk slate, lighter than the night sea and darker and greyer than the day sea, so a closed sea is one fogged colour on both. Its key (`MapScreen.tsx`) is a chip like the rest of the chrome, with a filled sample of the shade laid on the sea.
7. **The route's stops are keep-outs for names** (`ChartCanvas.tsx`): "Now", each numbered disc and its days join the list the chrome is already in, once per copy of the sheet. `GLYPH.pathStopDiscPx` is the disc's one size.
8. **The zoom column has one right edge** (`ViewControls.tsx`), and on a phone the fleets corner stops short of it (`FleetsCorner.tsx`).

9. **Past a coast, a harbour with no name on the glass is a quiet dot** (`src/chart/chartModel.ts` `quietHarbours`, read by `PortsLayer.tsx` and the roads): no triangle, ring, light or roads. Six to eight unnamed marks with their lamps were one grey smudge along Iberia, the Channel and Gibraltar. A harbour a fleet of yours is using and the pressed one keep their marks, and yours are painted last, over every neighbour. On a coast nothing changes; at the globe every city is still its lit dot (owner row 125).
10. **The shallows thin faster than the marks** (the cube of the scale) and fade with it, so a small island is not outlined like a sticker.
11. **A fleet of yours keeps the dark halo by day** (`LabelsLayer.tsx`): her brass name on the day sea's blue halo was the faintest name on the glass.
12. **"Now" stands over a disc on its own point** (`ShipPathLayer.tsx`): a route that ends where it began hid the word under its last stop.

## Decided by default (the owner may reverse)

- **The zoom readout is unchanged**, so on a phone it now bottoms at about 25 % (390 px) or 22 % (320 px) and not 0 %. The number is the owner's own scale (0 % the whole world, 47 % where every city is named); keeping it means "47 %" is the same water on every glass.
- **Merchants are hidden, not counted**, where the glass has no room. A count mark per sea would be a new sign with a new meaning; nothing asked for one.
- **A fleet of yours is never shrunk or thinned**, so furthest out she is the largest ship on the picture.
- **The harbour lights keep their brightness** (owner row 125: "cities to be bright light"); only their width follows the density.

## Left, and why

- **The coast is a coarse polygon at harbour zoom** (Portugal is five straight lines at 90 %). That is the 110 m coast file, not the drawing: a finer coast is a data slice of its own.
- **Named great ports still stand close at a strait** (Gibraltar, Cadiz, Seville at a sea's zoom): each has its name, so each keeps its mark.
- **The shade's raster has a row of sea cells under Antarctica**, a grey line at the foot of the furthest view with seas lit. It is the server's zone raster.
- **A name whose spot a route stop takes is dropped when no other side is free** (Lisbon, with "Now" on its roads). The route bar names every stop.
- **The other screens** were not reworked: the trays and pages opened from the map were looked at in the same walk and showed no fault of this kind; the fourth phone pass (`2026-11-14-lit-screens.md`) covered them the day before.

## Laws

`tests/map.look.spec.ts`, ten tests: the scale's bounds and evenness; the loosest view on portrait and landscape glasses, by clamp and by repeated zoom; who is thinned and that it is by the ship alone; which harbours are quiet dots; on the merchants-on world at 390 and 320 px, at the opening view, a sea and furthest out: no two names overlap, every halo is round, every merchant hull and harbour light is inside its bound for that density, the graticule is inside the world, a fleet of yours is on the picture, and furthest out the view is no taller than the world; on the lit-seas world at 320 and 390 px in English and Korean: the shade is painted before the harbours, hulls, rings and names, its key carries a filled sample, the open fleets list, the zoom column, the key and the minimap cover none of each other, and the zoom column has one right edge.

Six older specs asserted "the whole world" on a portrait phone and were moved with the rule: `map.marks` and `map.landfall` hold their world-view laws on a phone lying on its side (844 × 390) and the desk; `map.sun`'s globe test likewise; `map.goto` and `flags` accept the phone's own floor; `map.wrap` states both glasses.

## Proven, and not

Main's Acceptance run is the net for the rest; a spec that aims at an unnamed harbour's triangle at a sea's zoom would now find a dot.
