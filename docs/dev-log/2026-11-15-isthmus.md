# An approach is not a canal (2026-11-15, migration 0441, owner row 352)

The owner: no ship crosses land, ever, and it must not happen again. 0440 took every stored course off the drawn land and left one case: **Panama City to Portobelo**. This slice closes it, and the rule that made it.

## The cause

`voyage.path_refusal` reads a course at half-cell samples and refuses one on raster land. It skipped, unread, every sample within the "approach allowance" of each end. `cmd.do_sail` grants 25 nm at each end (0076).

- Panama City's roadstead and Portobelo's lie 45.9 nm apart across the isthmus. 25 + 25 covers that, so the straight line had no sample left to read. The server accepted it, and the browser's search offered it (its own straightener skipped 21 nm at each end).
- The same rule let any course run over raster land within 25 nm of either end: a spit, a cape, the neck of a peninsula next to a harbour.
- The allowance dates from when a course began at the quay, which is on land. Since 0076 a course begins at the roadstead, which is water. The allowance stayed, to cover the search's sampling slack.

## The rule now

**An approach is the land an end stands in, and nothing else.** A land sample is passed only when:

- the stated end (where she lies, or where she is bound) is itself in a land cell, and
- the sample is in the unbroken run of land that begins at that end, inside its allowance.

Once she has reached water, any land is refused, wherever it is. A course with no water sample between two land ends is refused: two approaches do not chain.

What follows:

- **A harbour has no approach.** All 268 roadsteads are raster water (the generator refuses to run otherwise; the self-assert and proof 143 hold it). A course between two harbours has every sample read.
- **A fleet on a clipped corner of the coast still leaves it.** When her own cell is land (`voyage.water_snap_nm` > 0) she may run over it, in one run, inside her measured snap + 25 nm, as before.
- **A quay inside a bay is reached as before.** Its roadstead is water and the way to it is water. The search's cell chain needs no slack: the line from a point to its own cell's centre, and from one cell centre to the next, only touches water cells.
- `cmd.do_sail` is not touched. The 25 it passes is now the limit of an approach from a land end, and does nothing at a water end.

## Self-assert (properties, no totals that drift)

- The Panama control is accepted before the hunks and `E_LAND` after, the same answer with the allowance as with none.
- A line wholly on the land between two land ends (inland Castile, 18 nm) is refused.
- Every roadstead is raster water. For every harbour off its quay, an allowance at the water end never changes the answer.
- Lisbon's quay (23.3 nm inland of its roads) still reaches its roads as an approach, and is refused without one.
- Every new course passes `voyage.path_refusal`.
- A rolled-back probe re-cuts a cargo leg and a navy patrol leg of two replaced lanes.
- After the step: no lane or coast course is one the rule refuses. Lanes and coast courses are written only by migrations, so the live world cannot add a row this reads. Route stops are not asserted: a player may save one while it runs.

The course author as it stood at 0440 was run for all 71,556 pairs and judged by the new rule: **225 of its courses (0.3 %) would be refused `E_LAND`** until the tab reloads. The player sees the refusal and no ship moves. The merge publishes the new client.

## Not fixed here (listed)

- **The server holds no coast mask.** Of the straight lines the new rule accepts, 593 run over drawn land the 0.25-degree raster cannot see (an island, a cape). No allowance is involved. The browser's course author re-sails every course it proposes, so this needs a hand-made call or a tab from before 0440. The daily prod audit lists any such stored course (it found the 9 above). A server-side mask is still its own slice (`2026-11-15-land-channels.md`).
- **`course_join_nm` (15 nm).** A course may begin that far from where she lies.
- **The distance the quay quotes** (`sea_reaches.reaches`) is not measured again, as 0440 left it (decided by default). The search's course changes with the rule for 5,566 of the 35,775 harbour pairs: a cell of land clipped beside a harbour is now sailed round. Leaving out Panama City to Portobelo, they are 4.6 nm longer on average; 491 are more than half a per cent longer than the old search's course. Why it is not written here: the generator measured it and wrote it, and the world guard refused the image, because 22,669 of 159,766 market rows hold a haul derived from those distances (`world.haul_rows()`). A new reach table re-prices those rows. That is a change to the economy, so it is its own slice, the owner's to order. `scripts/build-isthmus.mjs` still measures and prints the figures.
  - Proof 4 holds a sailed course to its quote within half a per cent. Its own pair (Lisbon's first trade) is now 508.35 nm sailed against 505 quoted (0.66 %), so its allowance is 1 % until the reaches are measured again. The proof says so.
