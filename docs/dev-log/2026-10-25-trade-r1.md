## 2. Decided by default (the owner may reverse any of them)

1. **The top-up buys to `public.npc_leg_days`** (the door's days rounded up, one day spare), not to the door's days exactly: a
   hull laden with the stores sails a shade slower (`ship_speed`'s load term), so a top-up to the exact figure can be refused
   by dust at the gate. The one-day spare is the chain's existing answer to the same question for merchants (0142). The margin
   itself is unchanged (`endurance_margin`, +15 %).
2. **The SAIL buys when there are tuns to take on, not when they cost something:** a short leg's stores can price at 0 d. and are still carried aboard.
3. **`supply_need` takes miles, not days:** the speed is read once, inside, so the board and the SAIL never measure one leg
   at two speeds.
4. **`sailable` reads `fits`.** The design added `supplies` without naming this; without it the board marks every leg longer
   than a new player's starting casks "shut" while the SAIL would sail it.
5. **Merchants' presets stay** (their planner writes them; nulling them would break `npc_plan`'s read of a loop's days).
   Humans' are nulled and backed up. R6 removes everything together.
6. **The card's units field stays; the max / min price fields are gone** (row 202). The draft keeps the fields for the
   route seed; from Trade they are always null.
7. **The route fold and the insured voyages stand under Orders** (the right pane on a wide glass; last on a phone).
