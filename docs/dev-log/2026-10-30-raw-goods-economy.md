## 0. What 0280 is, in one paragraph

Every good gains a **tier** (0 raw, 1–3 crafted) and a generated **is_raw**. The catalogue grows from
565 to **629 goods**: the design's **19 NEW** goods, plus **45 raw inputs** the design's own recipes
name but the catalogue never carried. **287 recipes** land as a table (766 ingredient rows). Each of
the **246 harbours** gets **1–2 production outputs** (464 rows): its highest-tier authored roster good
within a size ceiling, plus raw goods chosen so **every raw good in the catalogue has a producer**.
A city's **input demand** is derived, never stored: the recipe ingredients of what it makes, minus
what it makes itself. The old roster (`port_specialties`) and every price/quay function are
untouched; the quay will be re-cut to `city_production` in the sim slice, not here.

## 1. The exact schema change

| object | change |
|---|---|
| `public.goods.tier` | **NEW** `int not null default 0 check (tier between 0 and 3)`. 0 = raw material; 1–3 = crafted. The 565 existing goods take the design's tier. |
| `public.goods.is_raw` | **NEW** `boolean generated always as (tier = 0) stored`. The raw/crafted split is never a second authored fact. |
| `public.goods_recipes` | **NEW** `(product_id uuid, ingredient_id uuid, qty int 1–99, tier int 1–3)`, PK `(product_id, ingredient_id)`, `check (product_id <> ingredient_id)`, FKs to `goods` on delete cascade, index on `ingredient_id`. `tier` restates the product's `goods.tier` for the contract and is asserted equal; `goods.tier` is the authority. |
| `public.city_production` | **NEW** `(port_id uuid, good_id uuid, rank int 1–2)`, PK `(port_id, good_id)`, `unique (port_id, rank)`, FKs on delete cascade, index on `good_id`. 1–2 rows per harbour. |
| `public.city_inputs(uuid)` | **NEW**, server-only (no client grant). Derived `select distinct ingredient_id` over `city_production × goods_recipes`, minus the city's own outputs. |
| `public.city_wants_input(uuid, uuid)` | **NEW**, server-only. The one-good predicate over `city_inputs`; the shape the price sim will ask per row. |
| `port_goods` | **+15,744 rows**: 64 new goods × 246 harbours, seeded with 0005's own arithmetic (`world.affinity_at`, the same stock/target/production-rate formula) using `city_production` as the producer fact. `demand` is set from the one authority `world.demand_for`. Existing rows untouched. |

**Not changed:** `port_specialties` (1,288 offers, byte-identical), `port_offers`,
`port_offered_goods`, `world.mid_terms`, `world.price`, `world.quote`, `world.price_why`,
`public.restock_rate`, every drift/season/region-taste function. The self-assert captures their
definitions and raises if any moved.

### 2.2 The 45 raw inputs — a design gap, closed explicitly

The design's recipes name **45 ingredient codes that are not in the 565-good catalogue** (the RAW
sheet is a finer vocabulary than the catalogue). Every ingredient must resolve to a real good, and
the owner's model makes those raw inputs the things a city imports, so they are added as **tier-0
goods**:

`abalone, agave, apple, bamboo, bark, betel-leaf, cabbage, cacao-bean, cassava-root, cattle, clay,
coffee-bean, eel, feldspar, fresh-water, goose-feathers, grapes, gypsum, honeybush-leaf, lime, lychee,
mint, nipa, palm-fruit, palm-leaf, palm-sap, pandanus, pear, plum, quartz, raffia, ramie, rose, sand,
sea-cucumber, sesame, shark, shea-nut, shrimp, soybean, squid, stone, sugar-cane, tea-leaf,
witch-hazel-bark`.

**Base values are a documented default, not design data:** the RAW sheet carries none, so the
generator uses a category staple (food/marine 20, mineral 30, timber 25, fibre 30, animal 40,
medicine/dyestuff 40, crafts 40, water 4) and the catalogue's category bulk. **The owner may tune
these**; they are visible in `data/goods.json` as `baseValue: [v, v]`.

**`cassava` flips raw → crafted:** the existing `cassava` (Cassava Flour) is now tier 1 and has a
recipe (`2 cassava-root + 1 fresh-water`). `cassava-root` (tier 0) and `cassava-bread` (tier 2) give
it a real chain.

**Ten single-input recipes gained one process input.** The design had 10 recipes with one ingredient
(the six flours, cream, planks, malt, yarn), which violates the owner's 2–6 distinct-ingredient rule.
Each now carries `1 fresh-water` — the same process-input convention 97 other recipes already use.
The change is in `RECIPES.csv` and `README.md` so the design remains the one source.

## 6. How port goods were "filtered" — they were **not**, in 0280

The earlier plan ("filter port goods to raw-only") was replaced by the owner's refinement. 0280 is
**DATA ONLY**:

* `port_specialties` and `port_offers` still say what a city sells today; the quay's behaviour is
  byte-identical.
* `city_production` is the new authority for *what a city makes*. **The sim slice** re-cuts
  `public.port_offered_goods` to read it (one function; `port_offers` composes it already), which is
  the moment cities sell 1–2 things instead of their historical roster.
* Nothing here touches `world.price_why`, `world.mid_terms`, `world.quote` or `restock_rate`; the
  self-assert pins their definitions.

Why this order: the owner asked for the model, but a quay that sells 1–2 goods while prices still
come from the old 1,288-offer geography would be half a slice. Data first, then the sim, then the
client. The dev-log's §11 lists the exact next slice.

## 7. Real-time world region — what exists and what is missing

**Already in the chain (read, not changed by 0280):**

| mechanism | where | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `port_goods.drift` + `drift_slot_seconds`/`sigma`/`theta`/`clamp` | 0001/0010, live every minute | per-port OU price walk (the real-time noise) |
| `port_goods.season_mod` | 0001/0010 | per-port seasonal/fair modifier |
| `public.restock_rate(port)` | 0245 (E5), LIVE ON prod | restock rate from money invested in that city, set once a game-day |
| `city_wants` + daily draw | 0255 (E6b), LIVE | each city wants 1–3 goods it lacks, paid ×2 from its purse |
| `world.region_demand` / `world.region_taste` | 0066/0185 | a **static** region × category appetite (one of the price's terms) |
| `goods.origin_regions`, `world.haul_rows`, distance haul | 0062/0173 | region of origin and distance-to-source price gradient |

**Missing (this is the sim slice, deliberately not built here):**

1. A **real-time region-level supply/production factor** — today the drift/season are per port and the
   region taste is static; nothing makes a whole region's harvest, catch or harvest failure move
   together. The owner's "all should be considered as real-time world region" is this term.
2. The **input-demand premium** — `city_inputs` exists, but no price term reads it. The raw materials
   a city needs are not yet "sold at higher prices"; that is a `world.mid_terms` / `world.quote_priced`
   change.
3. **`port_goods.affinity` and rarity still read `port_specialties`** (the historical roster), not
   `city_production`. Prices therefore do not yet reflect the 1–2 production map; the sim slice must
   re-derive affinity (and decide whether rarity follows production).
4. `world.snapshot().goods[]` / `world.market().goods[]` do not yet serve `tier`/`is_raw`; the client
   cannot show raw vs crafted until that read (and the i18n words) lands.
5. The quay has not been re-cut to `city_production` (§6).

## 12. Risks for the closed economy

1. **The data is dark, so 0280 itself cannot break the closed economy.** It mints nothing, moves no
   money, and changes no existing price. The new market rows are priced, not funded.
2. **The Forge is a hard prerequisite for the sim slice.** If the quay is re-cut to
   `city_production` before `docs/CORP_STORAGE_FORGE.md` C3 lands and is lit, the **188 crafted goods
   with no city producer** (including all 19 new crafted goods) become unobtainable: they have
   recipes but no in-game maker. This is the single biggest sequencing risk. Either build the Forge
   first, or keep a fallback (e.g. crafted goods still sold where the historical roster sells them)
   until it is lit.
3. **Prices still come from the historical 1,288-offer geography.** The owner's "inputs sold at
   higher prices" is *not* live in 0280; the input-demand premium and the affinity re-derivation are
   the sim slice. A player would see no change today.
4. **The 1–2 offer model is a big route change.** When the quay flips, a city offers 1–2 goods, not
   4–10; trade routes must chain more ports, and the board/standing-route planner assumes broad
   rosters. Re-run `05_first_voyage_balance` under the new offer rule before lighting it.
5. **Invented numbers the owner may want to tune:** the 45 raw inputs' base values (category
   staples) and the 10 `fresh-water` process additions. Both are in the design source and easy to
   change; regenerate with `node scripts/build-raw-goods-migration.mjs`.
6. **The catalogue grows 565 → 629; the design said 584.** The extra 45 are the recipe inputs; this
   is stated in the migration header and this file. If the owner wants the 584 figure kept, the
   recipe sheet must be re-cut to catalogue codes instead — a design decision, not a generator one.
7. **New goods are not in `data/ports.json` rosters** (64 `check-ports` warnings). That is expected
   while `city_production` is the new authority; the sim slice must decide whether rosters become the
   production list (and update the world guard) or stay a historical record.
