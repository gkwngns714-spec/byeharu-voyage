# No course crosses the drawn land (2026-11-15, migration 0440, owner row 352)

The owner: *"some ships go pass through land - such as hadibu. i found one by myself, and i am guessing there are millions like this. fix this properly, and make procedures that this won't happen again"*.

## The root cause

There is no lane graph and no unchecked straight-line fallback. There is one rule, and it was blind to the land the player sees.

- The sea is a 0.25-degree raster (`public.sea_raster`, 0046), scan-filled from **Natural Earth 1:110m**. 1:110m has **no Socotra at all**: Hadibu's island is open sea to every rule in the game. It also lacks Bangka, Corfu's channel and most capes.
- The pathfinder (`src/lib/sea`) searches that raster, then **straightens** the cell chain: two vertices are joined whenever every half-cell sample between them is raster water. `voyage.path_refusal` judges the same line the same way. So every course was legal to the server.
- The chart draws **1:10m** (`public/coast`; the fine tiles since 2026-11-14). Every course was drawn straight over islands, capes and peninsulas that the raster cannot see.
- The owner's example is the stored lane **Hadibu to Kilwa: one straight line from the quay south across the whole island** (`[[12.65, 54.03], [-8.98, 39.52]]`).

## The audit (`scripts/db/audit-land-crossings.mjs`)

It runs on a world image, so it costs prod nothing. The land is the chart's own fine tiles at 1/80 degree, minus the cells the game carved open on purpose (the Thames, the Bosphorus). A crossing is a run over **2 nm** of that land (the chart's mid coast is itself simplified by 1.8 nm). A harbour's own approach is not a crossing: that is a run of 6 nm or less from a first or last point that is itself on land.

|---|---|---|
| lanes (`npc_courses`: merchants, cargo, pirates, navy) | **774 of 1,141** | 52 |
| route legs (`standing_route_stops`) | **2,422 of 3,350** | 145 (50 distinct) |

## The fix

- **`src/lib/sea/refine.ts`.** Each segment that runs over drawn land is searched again around the land, on a 1.5 nm grid whose cells are water by both rules (raster water, and not drawn land). The result is then straightened by line of sight under both rules. Every course it returns is raster water by the server's own rule (`courseIsWater`, the same sampling and the same 25 nm approach slack as `voyage.path_refusal`). If the result would be refused, the course comes back unchanged. The server's law is unchanged.
- **`public/coast/mask.json` (`src/lib/sea/coastMask.ts`, built by `scripts/build-coast-mask.mjs`).** It lists the 9,579 raster water cells in which the chart draws land, each with 20 x 20 bits (0.75 nm). It is 133 KB gzipped, fetched once after the world opens, and never fatal. It is stamped with the raster it was cut for.
- **What it costs a click.** Only the stretches around land are searched again: each cluster of land, with 20 nm of water either side, inside a box of at most 2 million fine cells. Then one bounded pass of line of sight runs over the whole course. Measured in Node over the 3,471 sampled harbour-to-harbour courses: mean **2 ms**, worst **40 ms** (Rio to Torshavn), on top of the search.
- **The browser.** `proposeCourse`, the one course author, re-sails every course once the mask has arrived (`SeaNav.coast`). The route builder, the trip planner, divert and sail all go through it.
- **Migration 0440** (generated by `scripts/build-clear-courses.mjs`). It re-sails 962 distinct stored courses, which are 0.40% longer on average. It rewrites, matched by `md5(course::text)`:
  - every lane (960), with its `nm`;
  - every route leg on that course (2,950), with its `course_nm`;
  - a cargo circuit's frozen pieces of that course, either way round;
  - the zones memo of the new courses.
  The self-assert checks three things:
  - (a) every new course passes `voyage.path_refusal`;
  - (b) the Hadibu to Kilwa control no longer has a sample in a box of solid Socotra, and its old course did;
  - (c) a probe cargo circuit of two replaced lanes, one of them backwards, is re-cut to exactly the new lanes' pieces. This probe is rolled back.
  Applied on the world image in PGlite in about 30 s.
- **Voyages already sailing are left to finish (decided by default).** A departed voyage's segments and frozen speeds are its schedule (0006, 0047), and re-cutting them would move a sailing ship and her arrival. Her next departure takes the new lane.

## What is left (listed, not hidden)

- **52 lanes and 50 route legs** still cross: Corfu's channel, the Hebrides and the North Channel, Stockholm's archipelago, Moon Sound, the Western Scheldt, and Jeju. The water they need is land to the 0.25-degree raster, so the fine search finds no way. They are listed in `scripts/db/land-crossings.known.json`, waiting for a raster slice that opens those channels.
- **57 roadsteads lie on drawn land**, Hadibu's among them: its quay is on the shore and the raster cell is open sea. Moving a roadstead changes `voyage.water_roadstead` and `sea_reaches`. That is its own slice.
- `sea_reaches` distances are still the raster search's figures. The re-sailed lanes are on average 0.40% longer than the figure quoted.

## The procedure, so it does not recur

- `tests/sea.noLandCrossing.spec.ts` runs on the build's own world on every PR that touches any of these:
  - the pathfinder, the refiner, the mask, the audit or the sea grid;
  - `ports.json`, `world-110m.json` or the coast index;
  - a migration that writes the lanes, the routes, the reaches, the raster or the cargo legs.

  The spec checks each of the following:
  - the mask is what the builder cuts for this raster;
  - every lane and route leg is off the drawn land, except the known list, which may only shrink;
  - the roadsteads are the known ones;
  - Hadibu to Kilwa does not cross Socotra (and the search alone still would, so the ruler sees the fault);
  - a fixed sample of 3,471 proposed courses crosses no more than 44 times.
- `docs/NAVIGATION_PLAN.md` section 8 gives the steps for adding a harbour, a lane, a channel, or a coast change. CLAUDE.md has the hard rule.
