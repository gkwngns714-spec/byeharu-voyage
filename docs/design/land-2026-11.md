## 1. Summary (two minutes)

**How it sounds: good.** It fits this game better than it first looks, because most of it is already written as data.

**One frame, many skills.**
- A skill is a Level (the kind the game has) plus a list of **jobs**. A job is one row: the level it asks, how long one takes, what it uses up, what it gives, the experience, and where it is done.
- Three kinds of job cover everything: **gather** (at a site), **make** (at home), **fight** (at a site).
- One function settles any job from the time that has passed. One screen draws any skill. So a new skill is rows, words and a mark. No new logic, no new screen.
- The content is in the catalogue already: 342 raw goods and 287 recipes (R). Iron, coal, cod, timber, hides, wheat; iron bars, steel, salt cod, leather, bread. Each has an English and a Korean name and a price in 254 markets. The land game is how a company gets and makes these with its own hands.

**The roster.** Twenty-two skills. Section 5 has each one with its loop, what it uses and what it feeds.

| | First five (a closed loop) | Later, in this order |
|---|---|---|
| Gather | Mining · Fishing | Logging · Hunting · Farming · Herbs |
| Make | Smithing · Cooking | Carpentry · Leatherwork · Gunsmithing · Apothecary · Weaving · Brewing · Jewellery |
| Fight | Arms | Aim · Guard |
| Support | | Scouting · Bounties · Digs · Surveying |

**The loop, in three sentences.** You send your party to a named site, and it works there for as long as you are away, up to twelve hours; what it brings home waits for you. At home the party turns ore into bars, and bars into tools and arms. Tools make the work faster, arms let the party hunt and work the wild sites, and those give what the next step needs.

**What the gear is for.** The party itself. A tool speeds the skill it belongs to, from the first hour. Arms and armour decide the party's fights, and the fights give hides, game and the way into richer sites. The fights run on the fight the game already has (`public.tick_fight`); there is no second combat system. Selling is the third use: what the party digs and makes are ordinary goods, sold in the markets that exist.

**What "one shared world" changed.** A copy of Melvor Idle would be a private game in a browser. This is not that:

| | Melvor Idle | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Where the work is | Your own rocks | Named sites every company uses. You see who is there. Company adds a little; nobody takes from anybody (RuneScape removed the fight over rocks in 2019, section 2.2). |
| Time | Your own clock | One clock. Progress is real time passed, for everyone, at most 12 hours away. Nothing sold can skip time. |
| Goods | A private bank | The world's goods: same names, same storage, same markets, same coin. Cities and the 845 merchant houses are the other traders, so it works with one player. |
| Others | None | Boards for each skill, each site's works filled by everyone, rich veins announced in the News for all, later a boss with one shared health. No PvP. |
| Who works it out | Your own browser | The server, from the time passed. It writes 4 to 6 rows when you press Collect and none while you are away. |

**The first three slices** (section 8). Each is about a day; the first is a day and a half.

| Slice | What ships | Fun or useful by itself |
|---|---|---|
| L1 The party digs | The frame, the Land tab, Mining, two sites, who is there, 12 hours away | A party that brings iron and coal home while you sail or sleep; a new Level with things to open |
| L2 The smith | Smithing: iron bars, steel, an iron pick | The first thing made by hand, and it makes the digging faster |
| L3 The hunt | Arms: blade, shield, armour, four foes | Gear has a job; the first fights a player will have had |

Then Fishing and Cooking arrive as rows only: the proof that a skill costs half a day.

**My honest judgment** (section 9). Build it, beside the three small sea fixes of the content plan, not instead of them. It gives the game a second loop with real choices and a long ladder, and it fills the hour at sea in which there is nothing to press. It does not mend the sea loop, and its stakes are soft at first. The danger is breadth with no use: RuneScape's own rework filled the mines and left the forges empty [R5]. So a skill ships only when something uses what it makes.

**Four defaults to look at first** (section 10 has all eighteen):
1. What the party digs and makes are the world's goods, in the Storage and markets that exist. No ship is ever needed. (The other shape, a sealed land with its own things, is in section 9.)
2. Iron first, not bronze: in this world's prices bronze costs three times iron.
3. Levels stay 1 to 20, on the game's one curve. A skill takes about 100 hours of work to top; first times make the first hour quick.
4. A recipe that a skill owns is no longer made at once at the Forge. That changes the Production page for those recipes.

### 2.2 The shared world: fight over it, or share it?

- **RuneScape took the fight out.** Before 2019 one player's ore emptied the rock for everyone. The rework: "One of the main aims of the rework for Mining was to reduce competition over rocks" [R3]. Jagex's own words: "Rather than fleeing to another world when you see another player, you can mine together in peace" [R4].
- **Old School RuneScape went further in 2023.** Trees now fall on a timer, not on a roll, and "Each player will receive a +1 boost to Woodcutting for each player at the tree, up to a maximum of +10 with ten players". The stated aim: "a more social experience" by "removing resource competition" [O1].
- **Shared events on a clock.** A shooting star lands on each world about every 90 minutes and everyone mines the one rock. Since 2023 stars "no longer deplete faster when more people are mining them" [O2].
- **What idle mining did to the market.** Jagex, ten months after the rework: "The release of the rework saw a huge and sustained increase in players Mining, but no similar increase in players Smithing", and "The main reason for the low value of ore is the rate at which it can be produced through AFK Mining" [R5]. They cut the idle rate hard.
- **The exchange.** Players post offers and the game matches them: "Trades succeed when one player's buy offer is greater than or equal to another player's sell offer". Buying is limited by item every 4 hours. A fee of 2 % on a sale takes coin out of the game [O3]. It needs a crowd on both sides.
- **Boards.** "players are ranked by their experience in skills", and a skill is listed from level 15 [R9].
- **Idle games that are shared** (Milky Way Idle, Idle Clans): each player's rocks are private. What is shared is the market (a 4 % fee in Milky Way Idle, which also pays for bonuses that "benefit all players" [I2][I3]), clans, party fights and boards. Time away stops at 10 hours and at 12, and more is sold [I1][I4].

What I take: no contest over a vein; company is a bonus; events on the shared clock; idle yield kept low on purpose; the market that exists, not a new exchange.

### 2.3 Sending a party: what makes it a choice and not a timer

| Game | The choice at sending | What went wrong with it |
|---|---|---|
| RuneScape, player-owned ports | "Only 3 voyages are displayed at any one time." "The chance of a successful voyage is equal to the least filled indicator" of Morale, Combat and Seafaring. 15 minutes to many hours. A failure can damage the ship or kill crew [R8]. | It became a round: "millions of coins per week for 5 minutes of gameplay per day" [R12]. |
| World of Warcraft, the mission table | Pick followers "able to counter the abilities of the mission's target enemies". 30 minutes to 10 hours [D1]. | "a tedious, frequent chore that you couldn't escape" [D2]. A later version hid the odds, and players hated that [D3]. |
| Final Fantasy XIV, retainer ventures | 1 hour or 18 hours; better gear brings more back [D4]. | No way to fail: a button to press again [D4]. |
| Fallout Shelter | How long to stay out: "The longer the exploration lasts, the more dangerous enemies will your dweller meet". Coming home takes half the time out, and is safe [D5]. | Small pay for the waiting: "truly tiny payoffs" [D8]. |
| Dragon Age: Inquisition, the war table | Which agent, against a clock. | "There's no failure for these Operations" [D6]: colour, not play. |

Four rules follow, and section 6.2 keeps them:
1. **Show the odds and the haul before sending.** Hidden odds were the worst-liked version.
2. **The player picks the length.** A chosen wait is "an appointment with herself" [D7].
3. **Something can be lost.** With nothing at stake it is a button.
4. **Never punish absence.** Twelve hours covers a night or a working day, and nothing rots after that. Jagex cut its own daily rounds in March 2026 because they were "perceived as mandatory" [R14].

### 2.4 What carries the fun

- **Carries:** the repeating job that goes on while away; the summary on return; the next unlock always in sight; one skill feeding another; rare finds; gear that changes what you can do.
- **Padding:** rocks that run out; mastery's long tail; the last third of a 99-level curve; bank slots as friction; anything that needs a click to stay efficient.
- Players' own words for the good part: "It's like RuneScape except you don't have to play it"; "Low time investment, high fun yield" [M4].

## 4. The frame: what a skill is

### 4.1 The model

**A skill** is one row of `public.level_skills` and a list of jobs.

**A job** is one row of a new table, `public.land_actions`. It says:

| Field | Meaning | Iron | Iron Bars | Wolves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `skill` | whose Level it asks and raises | mining | smithing | arms |
| `kind` | gather, make or fight | gather | make | fight |
| `level` | the Level it asks | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| `seconds` | how long one takes, before tools | 45 | 40 | 180 |
| `xp` | experience for one | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| `site` | where; empty means at home | Forest of Dean | (home) | The Ardennes |
| what it gives | a good, an item, or a foe | 1 Iron | 1 Iron Bars | the foe's finds |
| `tool` | the tool that speeds it | pick | hammer (later) | (none) |

- **What a job uses is never typed twice.** A made good reads its recipe from `public.goods_recipes`. A made item reads `public.item_recipes`. A recipe keeps its one authority.
- **Rare finds** are rows of `public.land_drops`: the job, the thing, one in how many.
- **A foe** is a row of `public.land_foes`: the figures the fight needs.
- **A party** is one row of `public.land_parties`: which job, where, since when. That one row is all the state the idle game keeps for a company.

Three kinds are enough for every skill in section 5:

| Kind | What it is | Skills |
|---|---|---|
| gather | No inputs. At a site. Gives a raw good. | Mining, Fishing, Logging, Hunting, Herbs. Farming too, with a field that works by itself: a rule of its own (4.3). |
| make | Inputs from Storage, by the recipe. At home. Gives a good or an item. | Smithing, Cooking, Carpentry, Leatherwork, Weaving, Brewing, Apothecary, Gunsmithing, Jewellery |
| fight | A bout against a foe. At a site. Gives loot. | Arms, Aim, Guard; Bounties |

### 4.2 One settle, one screen

- **`public.land_settle(party, now)`** is the one writer of everything a job earns. It counts how many of the job were finished in the time passed, rolls the rare finds, and books goods, items and experience through the writers that exist (`public.storage_put`, `public.storage_take`, `public.level_grant`). It knows kinds, not skills. Section 6.5 has the arithmetic.
- **`SkillFace`** is one client component. It draws any skill from what `world.land()` serves: the jobs, each with its level, time, experience, what it uses against what you have, and whether the server will take it. The Levels tiles already work this way (`src/live/LevelTiles.tsx:54`: it maps over what is served).

### 4.3 What a new skill costs, once the frame stands

| The skill | What is added | Time (J) |
|---|---|---|
| Gathers or makes goods the catalogue has (Fishing, Cooking, Logging, Carpentry, Leatherwork, Weaving, Brewing) | Rows: 1 in `level_skills`, 2 in `level_activities`, its jobs in `land_actions`, a site or two. A mark in `MARKS`. Its name and its sites' names in English and Korean. A line in the proof. | Half a day |
| Also makes new items (Gunsmithing, Jewellery) | The same, and rows in `item_kinds`, `item_recipes`, `item_effects`. | Half a day to a day |
| Brings a new rule (Farming's fields, Bounties, Digs, Surveying) | One slice: one table or one new kind of job. | A slice |

No new migration of logic for the first two rows of that table, and no new screen for any.

### 4.4 Can `public.level_skills` carry this? Yes. Three small changes, no second level system.

1. **A group.** A new column `side`, `sea` or `land`, served by `public.level_skills_card`, so the You page draws two groups of tiles.
2. **No limit a day for a land skill.** Today a granted level must have a daily limit (the table's check at 0411:148), 300 for all of them. That limit guards acts that take no time. A job takes time, so its own clock is the limit. The check is loosened so that `day_cap` may be empty. `public.level_grant` already does the right thing with an empty limit (0411:345: in Postgres `least` ignores a null).
3. **Two activity rows a skill** in `public.level_activities`. One is the work ("Work at a mine", 1 for 1): the settle passes the experience of the turns done, and each job's own figure is on its row and on the skill's screen. The other is a first time ("A job done, or a site worked, for the first time": 25, once each), paid once by its key, the way a ship built pays 60 once (0411:197).

What it cannot carry, said plainly:
- **The jobs.** It lists levels, not what there is to do. That is the new table `land_actions`. It is a list of work, not a level system.
- **A hundred levels.** The one curve stops at 20 (`least(20, ...)`, 0069:111). RuneScape's 99 is not on offer without lifting the top for EVERY level, the ships' and captains' too: a ship and a captain level on the same function (0127:222, 0160:331), and the fight reads those levels. **Default: 20 stays.** A second curve for land would be a second level system. If the owner wants 99, that is its own slice for all levels at once.

**The pace this gives.** A job pays about 1.4 experience a minute of work, whatever its level (a slower job pays more for each).

| Level | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 15 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Experience | 25 | 100 | 225 | 400 | 1,225 | 2,025 | 3,025 | 4,900 | 9,025 |
| Work time | 18 min | 1 h 11 | 2 h 41 | 4 h 46 | 14 h 35 | 24 h | 36 h | 58 h | 107 h |

- **A skill tops out after about 100 hours of work.** A party that works round the clock on one skill does that in five days; in ordinary play it is two or three weeks. The roster of 22 is about 2,400 hours: months.
- **Why so long.** The owner's own rule for levels: "levelling is a long grind" (2026-10-15, `docs/SHIPS_AND_GEAR.md`: a ship reaches level 20 after about five months at sea). The eight levels granted by doing are held to 300 a day, a month each at the least. A party works day and night, so a quick pace would top a skill in a day.
- **Why the first hour is still quick.** First times pay 25 each. The first haul of iron is two of them (the job, the site): Mining 2 at once. A skill holds about twenty first times, 500 experience in all, most of them early.
- The dial is one figure a skill (`level_activities.per`): 2 doubles every time above.
- The level is not the only ladder. The metal is another: each step up costs several of the step below (6.3). Later, the marks for each job.

**Reputation.** Every level reached pays Reputation, 10 x the level, as all levels do (0411:356-365). A land skill taken to 20 pays 2,090, like any other. The 22 land skills would pay 45,980 in level gifts, against 22,990 for all eleven levels today. **Default: leave the one rule.** The owner's dial is `rep_level_per_level`.

### 5.3 The eleven levels that exist, beside these

|---|---|---|

**The Forge.** Today the Forge makes any made good at once, for no skill, from goods stored at a city where a ship lies (0281). ### 5.4 The order to ship

The lead's guess was Mining, Smithing, Fishing, Cooking and a first fight. I agree with the five, in this order:

1. **Mining**, with the frame (L1).
2. **Smithing** (L2). The loop's first turn closes: the pick speeds the digging.
3. **Arms** (L3). The loop closes: gear has its job.
4. **Fishing and Cooking** together (L4), as rows. Food makes a hunt last.

Arms comes before food because the question "what is the gear for" should not stay open for two slices. A hunt without food is short, and that is fine for a first one.

After that: the shared layer (works, boards, rich veins), then Logging with Carpentry, Hunting with Leatherwork, Farming, Aim with Gunsmithing, Guard, Scouting, Herbs with Apothecary, Bounties, and the rest when something asks for them.

## 6. The design, decided

### 6.1 The core loop

**In one paragraph.** A company has a party ashore, based in the city where it was founded. The player gives the party one job: dig iron at the Forest of Dean, smelt bars at home, hunt wolves in the Ardennes. The party does that job over and over, by the clock, whether the player is there or not, for up to twelve hours. Each visit the player collects what came home, sees a level rise or a thing open, and chooses the next job by what the next tool or blade needs. Tools make jobs faster. Arms open hunts and wild sites. Those give what the next metal needs. No ship is needed at any step.

**The first 10 minutes** (with L1 to L3 live; all times J):

| Clock | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Open Land. "Party · at home". Mining lists Iron and Sea Coal at the Forest of Dean. Copper is grey: "Mining 1 / 3". |
| 0:10 | Press Iron. A tray says what the server will do: "6 a trip · 5:00 a trip · 1 experience each · first time +25 · 2 companies there: +4 %". Press Send. |
| 0:25 | "Digging 1 / 6". Go back to Trade and sail as usual. |
| 5:10 | "Home · 6 Iron". Collect: two first times (Iron, the Forest of Dean), +50, and 6 for the work. Mining 2, +20 Reputation. The party is out again by itself. |
| 5:30 | Change the job to Sea Coal: the smith will want it. |
| 10:00 | "Home · 6 Sea Coal". Collect: a first time (Sea Coal), +25, and 6 for the work. Smithing shows "Iron Bars · 2 Iron + 1 Sea Coal · you have 6 and 6". Press Make, 3. |

Slower than Melvor's first minute, on purpose: the first hauls arrive while the ship is on her first voyages, and the first ten minutes at sea are 7.5 minutes of nothing to press (content plan 3.2). The two loops fill each other's waits.

**The first session** (about an hour):
- About minute 12: three Iron Bars. Smithing 2, by the first time.
- About minute 14: the Iron pick. Digging is 8 % faster.
- About minute 30: Mining 3. Copper opens at Cornwall.
- Minute 30 to 50: more bars, an Iron blade. The first hunt: Wolves. Arms 2 at the first win. Hides come home.
- Before leaving: Repeat is on. A mule for 3,000 coin: 10 a trip instead of 6.
- A new thing about every ten minutes, and each one is a choice of what the party does next.

**The first week:**
- Day 2: Smithing 5, Steel. Mining 5, Tin. Each is under five hours of the party's work.
- Day 3 or 4: a steel pick and a steel blade. Arms 5: steel can be worn. Boar, then Brigands.
- Day 5 to 7: Mining near 10, Smithing and Arms near 8. A richer, further mine. Fish and salt cod for longer hunts. The first wild site.
- The rhythm: two visits a day lose nothing. More visits get more done, because each visit can change the job.

### 6.2 Expeditions

**A site** is a row: a name, what is there, how far, how wild. A list, not a map. The first three:

| Site | What is there | Opens at | March | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Forest of Dean | Iron, Sea Coal | Mining 1 | 0:15 | safe |
| Cornwall | Copper, Tin | Mining 3, Mining 5 | 0:45 | safe |
| The Ardennes | Wolves, Boar, Brigands, a Bear | Arms 1, 3, 6, 10 | 0:45 | a hunting ground |

Later sites, each a real place of the age: Dannemora (iron, richer), Newcastle (coal), Falun (copper), the Mendips (lead), Sicily (sulphur), Bengal (saltpetre), Setubal's salt pans, Kutna Hora (silver), Almaden (quicksilver), Golconda (diamonds, as rare finds), the Gold Coast (gold, wild), Potosi (silver, wild), the Grand Banks (cod), the Dogger Bank (herring), Norway's woods (pine and masts).

The march is the site's own, the same for every company. A party does not walk from Lisbon to Sweden: the company keeps people there. Distance on the sea chart plays no part, by the owner's word that this is not the ships' game.

**A party** is the company's people ashore: one party to begin with. It has packs (how much one trip carries), tools, and from L3 a weapon, a shield and armour. It does one job at a time. A second party is a late, dear purchase.

**How long.** One trip is: march out, do the job until the packs are full, march home.

- Iron at the Forest of Dean, 6 in the packs: 0:15 + 6 x 0:45 + 0:15 = 5:00.
- With Repeat on (the default) the party goes straight out again, until the player has been away 12 hours.
- A haul is home when its trip is home. Collect books everything that has come home.
- Call home ends the job. The party walks back with what is in its packs and is home after one march. A new job begins when it is home.

**What it brings back.** The good of the job, one a turn; now and then a rare find; experience for each turn. Goods go to the company's Storage in its home city: the same Storage the HQ's Production page shows.

**What level and tools change.**

| Thing | What it does |
|---|---|
| The skill's Level | Opens jobs and sites. Later its steps (`level_perks`) add a chance of a second unit. |
| The tool (a pick for Mining) | Each metal makes its job 8 % faster: iron 8 %, steel 16 %, up to 40 %. The party uses the best it owns. Nothing to equip. |
| Packs | 6 a trip. Mules add: 10, 15, 20, 30, for 3,000, 8,000, 20,000 and 50,000 coin, paid to the home city. Bigger packs waste less time marching. |
| Company there | +2 % for each other company at the site when the party arrives, to +10 %. |
| Arms, armour, food | Decide hunts and wild sites (6.4). |

Every bonus to a haul is a chance of a second unit on a turn, rolled by the dice. Goods stay whole numbers.

**What can go wrong.**

| What | Where | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Storage is full | Any job | Nothing is lost. What does not fit waits with the party and is booked when there is room. While a haul waits, the party is not sent again: make bars or sell first. |
| The party is beaten | A hunt; later a wild site | It comes home. Half of that trip's loot is lost. It rests ten minutes. |
| Out of food | A long hunt (L4) | It comes home early with what it has. |
| A good cannot be made | Smithing with too little ore | The job stops at the last one it could make. |

No tool, blade or armour is ever lost in the first version. Melvor takes one worn item on a death. **Default: not here, not yet.** Honestly: at a safe mine nothing can go wrong but a full Storage. The stake arrives with hunts, and grows with wild sites.

**The decision in it.** Five choices. They keep the four rules of section 2.3:

1. **What to work.** What the next tool or blade needs. What sells best at home today. Where a rich vein was announced.
2. **Near or far.** A far or wild site pays more a turn and costs more marching. It is worth it only with big packs.
3. **What to carry.** Food rides in the packs. More food is a longer hunt and a smaller haul.
4. **How bold.** The party's order on a hunt: fall back at a quarter of its health lost, at half, or fight to the end.
5. **The odds are shown.** The Send tray is the server's own answer: time a trip, haul a trip, experience, and for a hunt the party's wins in the last ten bouts against that foe.

### 6.3 Minerals and the ladder

**Iron first, not bronze.** RuneScape and Melvor start with copper and tin. In this world's price list copper is 90 to 170 and tin 140 to 260, and iron is 40 to 80 (R: `data/goods.json`, base values). Bronze cost three times iron in 1600 too. A ladder that started dear and went cheap would be upside down. So the party's ladder is the steels, and copper and tin come soon after for their own uses.

**The first version: one chain.** Iron and Sea Coal (Mining 1) make Iron Bars (Smithing 1), which make a pick, a blade, a shield and an armour. That is slices L1 to L3, and it adds no good to the catalogue.

**Mining, in full.** All are goods today except the last.

| 1 | Iron | The Forest of Dean; later Dannemora | 0:45 | 1 | Smithing |
| 2 | Clay | Cornwall | 0:40 | 1 | Wootz steel |
| 3 | Copper | Cornwall; later Falun | 1:30 | 2 | Brass, guns, the market |
| 5 | Tin | Cornwall | 2:00 | 3 | Bronze, pewter |

Gems (diamonds, emeralds, sapphires, amber) are rare finds on any turn, likelier at the deeper sites.

**How fast, and why so slow beside Melvor.** One unit here is one unit of cargo, worth real coin at a real market. At the times above, an hour of iron is 80 units, about 4,800 coin at the middle of the catalogue's band, before the market answers a seller. That is deliberate. Jagex's own finding: "The main reason for the low value of ore is the rate at which it can be produced through AFK Mining" [R5]. **The builder sets the seconds** by one rule: a party-hour of the first ores should sell at home for 3,000 to 5,000 coin after the market's own answer, and the last ores for about three times that.

**The five metals.** The first three bars and their recipes exist today.

| Metal | The bar | Its recipe | Smithing to make | Arms to use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Iron | Iron Bars | 2 Iron + 1 Sea Coal | 1 | 1 |
| Steel | Steel Billets | 3 Iron Bars + 2 Sea Coal | 5 | 5 |
| Wootz | Wootz Steel | 3 Iron Bars + 2 Sea Coal + 1 Clay | 10 | 10 |

- Each metal makes four things: a pick (3 bars), a blade (3), a shield (4), an armour (6). Later an axe, a net weight, a hammer: a tool for each skill.

- Sky iron is found, never dug at will: the genre's rule that the best metal is a drop. Emperor Jahangir had blades forged from a fallen star in 1621 [H1]; the name is of this world.
- Bronze and brass are a side branch for guns and fine goods (Gunsmithing, Jewellery), not a rung.

### 6.4 Making gear, and what the gear is for

**Recipes.** A bar is a good, made by its row in `goods_recipes`. A pick or a blade is an item kind, made by its rows in `item_recipes`. Smithing's jobs point at those rows. The smith's Level opens them: an Iron pick at 1, a blade at 2, a shield at 3, an armour at 4, Steel Billets at 5.

**Quality and mastery. Default: neither in the first three slices.**
- Later, **marks**: the count of a job done, on the one curve, twenty marks a job. Each few marks add a chance of a second unit, or of keeping a bar. This is Melvor's mastery, kept short, because its long tail is what its own players call useless (2.1).
- Later still, **wear**: the game already wears a ship's fittings (0158). The same rule on a party's arms gives bars a use that never ends.
- Not planned: grades of quality. The letters S A B C are the captains'.

**What the gear is for. Decided: the party.**

| Use | From | Why it is enough |
|---|---|---|
| **A tool speeds its skill** | L2 | Felt in the first twenty minutes. Every skill gets one. |
| **Arms and armour decide the party's fights** | L3 | Hunts give hides and game. Wild sites give the dear minerals. A boss gives the last metal. Each needs better steel than the one before. |
| **Selling** | L1 | Bars, nails and blades are goods. The markets and the houses that exist buy them. |
| A ship's gear | Not planned | The owner's word. But it is true, and free, that the Workshop's fittings and the yard's hulls ask for iron, timber and nails. A company with a smith ashore will have them. |

Gear itself (the pick, the blade) is not traded between players at first. A company makes its own. That keeps Smithing worth doing while the world is small.

**Land fights, the smallest fun version.**

- A **hunting ground** is a site. Its jobs are foes: Wolves (Arms 1), Boar (3), Brigands (6), a Bear (10).
- One turn of the job is one **bout**: three minutes, most of it tracking and binding up; the fight inside it is the game's own, 13 to 72 seconds on the fight's clock (`docs/design/tick-combat.md` 2).
- The party has **health**. It carries from bout to bout. At home it comes back by itself. On a hunt, food from the packs restores it (L4).
- A bout won pays 4 experience and the foe's finds. A bout lost pays 1, and the party goes home.
- **Falling back is not being beaten.** A party that falls back at its order comes home with all it has taken. A party fought down to nothing loses half (6.2). So the order is the risk the player sets: fall back early and keep little, or press on for more.

**One fight engine.** A bout is one call of `public.tick_fight(seed, a, b, opts)` (0435:154). It is a pure function of two lists of hulls. A party and a foe are written as hulls:

| The fight asks for | A party gives | A foe gives |
|---|---|---|
| `hull`, `maxd` | Health now, health at most (by Arms level and armour) | Its own, from `land_foes` |
| `attack`, `accuracy` | The weapon's, plus the Arms level | Its own |
| `reach` | The weapon's: a blade short, a pike middling, a bow or musket long. The fight already lets only the longer reach fire in its first windows. | Its own |
| `armour` | The armour's and the shield's | Its own |
| `crew`, `tier` | The party's hands; the metal's step | Its own. A pack of wolves is several small hulls: the fight is N against M already. |
| `sighting` | 10; later the Scouting level | Its own |

- The party is the side that closes. The foe is the side set upon.
- **One change to the engine:** the side that closes breaks off at 15 % of its strength lost, a knob made for raiders (`fight_raider_break`, 0189:133). A hunt needs the player's own order instead. So `p_opts` gains one optional key, `b_break`. Absent, a sea fight is to the digit what it was; the slice proves that on frozen sheets.
- **No battle row.** `public.battle_open` holds fleets still, calls a navy and draws a ring on the map. None of that is true of a hunt. A bout's result is booked by the land settle. A bout can still be watched: it is re-run from its seed, and its lines are the battle log's own (`src/live/battle/lines.ts:39`), with land words for the eight moves.
- **So there is one fight, in two places.** Any change to how a blow lands changes both, which is the point.
- Later, the same way the sea got it: the player acts in a bout by hand (the action bar, `cmd.battle_act`'s model). That is how a boss is fought (6.6).

**Before any foe is lit** the builder fights 100 bouts on frozen sheets for each, as the pirate slices did. The aim (J): a party in the metal of its level wins about eight in ten against the foe of its level, and loses to the next.

What I read and what I did not: the engine's inputs and rules at 0435 (R). Its body has been re-cut since by 0439, 0450 and 0453; I did not read those bodies. The builder slices the live one.

### 6.5 Idle and offline: how the server works it out

The hard rule (`docs/DATABASE_BUDGET.md`): no job that writes for every player every tick. So there is no land job at all.

**The state** is the party's one row: the job, its terms, when it began, when the player last pressed, how many turns are already booked.

**The terms are frozen at Send:** seconds a turn `a` (with the tool), the march `m`, the packs `p`, the bonus for company. A new pick changes the next Send, not the job that is running. That is what makes the sum below the same whenever it is asked.

**The sum.** With `e` the real seconds since the job began, stopped at 12 hours after the player's last press:

```
trip      = 2 x m + p x a
trips     = floor(e / trip)                          trips that are home
in packs  = min(p, floor((e - trips x trip - m) / a))  never below 0
home      = trips x p                                turns whose goods are home
```

A make at home has no march: `done = min(asked, floor(e / a))`.

This is an old rule of browser games: work out the time passed when someone asks, not on a timer [K4]; and "Calculate offline the same as online" [K3].

**A read writes nothing.** `world.land()` does that sum, a few multiplications, and serves it: what is home, what is in the packs, when the party is next home. The countdown on the screen is drawn from the served instant, as the arrival countdown is. A hunt is the one job a read cannot count: how many bouts were fought depends on how they went. For a hunt the read says how long the party has been out, and a press says what happened.

**A press settles.** Send, Collect, Call home, and every other land door call `public.land_settle` first. It books the turns not yet booked:

- goods: `public.storage_put`, one row a good (it adds to the row that is there);
- experience: `public.level_grant`, with a key (`land:<job>:<trip>`), so settling twice pays once. Under 10 experience is carried to the next press (section 7). A first time is its own grant, with its own key (`new:<job>`, `new:<site>`);
- rare finds: one roll a turn, `voyage.rng_raw(job, turn, 'find', secret)`. The same job, turn and secret give the same roll, whenever and however often it is asked. This is the rule a voyage's days already keep ("offline settlement byte-identical to tick-by-tick", 0006:135-138);
- a hunt: its bouts in order, each one engine call seeded by (job, bout), health carried from one to the next. At most 200 bouts a press.

**The one clock.** Progress is real time and nothing else. Nothing in the game, bought or earned, moves a party's clock forward. A tool makes a turn shorter from the next Send on; that is speed, earned and visible, not a skip. Every company lives the same hours. The owner's clock knob (`public.clock_set`, 0413) moves the ships and the markets; a party's seconds are a person's pacing, like skill training, and do not move with it (decided by default, as 0413 decided for training).

**The cap: 12 hours** from the player's last press (`land_away_hours`). Melvor allows 24; the shared idle games allow 10 and 12 and sell more (2.2). Twelve covers a night or a working day. After it the party waits where it stopped, and goes on from there at the next press. Nothing is lost, and nothing piles up beyond half a day.

**Rows written.**

| The press | Rows |
|---|---|
| Send | 1 (the party) |
| Start a make | 1, and 1 for each ingredient taken from Storage |
| Collect | 1 party, 1 to 3 Storage, 1 `level_xp`, 1 `level_book`: 4 to 6. Later 3 more (a mark, the site's works, the week's board). |
| A level reached | 2 or 3 more (Reputation, the event) |
| Away, or looking | 0 |

Section 7 turns that into rows a day and a minute, for 1, 100 and 1,000 companies.

### 6.6 The shared world

Decided, one by one.

| Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Do veins run out for everyone? | **No.** Nobody can dig a site empty. | RuneScape removed exactly this (2.2). And a lazy settle cannot be fair about a shared pile: who collects first would win. |
| Is a site's yield fought over? | **No. Company adds:** +2 % for each other company there when you arrive, to +10 %. | Old School's Forestry rule. It is frozen at Send, so it costs nothing to compute. |
| Who is there? | **A list, in text:** each company at the site, its level in that skill, what it works, since when. | It is a read of the party rows. No presence writes, no heartbeat. A row older than 12 hours is not "there". |
| A site's works | **One bar a site, filled by everyone's turns.** Each step is +2 % for all, to +10 %. A thin trickle fills it even when nobody digs. | A goal the whole world shares. One row a site. |
| The week's best | **"Best here": the company with the most turns at a site this week.** The week is a key, not a wipe (`docs/WEEKLY_ROLLOVER.md`). | Rivalry with nothing taken from anyone. |
| Shared events | **A rich vein:** a site's job pays half again for three hours. In the News before it starts, for everyone. | On the fair's calendar (`buff_kinds`), which exists. RuneScape's sparkling rock, made a world event. |
| The economy | **The markets that exist, and the one coin.** No Grand Exchange. | Below. |
| Boards | **A board for each skill,** by experience, real companies only. Shown from five companies up; under that, your own line. | `level_xp` already holds it. An empty board is a sad thing to show. |
| Fighting with others | **A boss with one health for the whole world,** fought by hand, one bout a press. Loot by share of the harm done. No parties of several companies yet. | Later, when there are companies to share it. One row a boss. A group needs a crowd to form; the boss does not. |
| Fighting each other | **No. PvP stays off.** | The game's standing rule, and nothing here needs it. |
| Offline time | **Real time, capped, the same for all** (6.5). | The owner's "same timeline". |

**The economy, decided: one authority, the one that exists.**

- What the party digs and makes are goods. They sit in Storage. They sell at the price every harbour already keeps (`world.price`, 0005:367), to a city that pays from its own purse. Coin moves; none is made (the closed economy holds).
- **The other traders are already here.** 845 merchant houses carry goods from where they are cheap to where they are dear. A company that pours iron into Lisbon lowers Lisbon's price, and the houses carry it off. That is a working market with one player. Koster, on small worlds: "it's not literal population that matters. It's economic participation" [K1].
- **The cities are the buyer of last resort.** "Having an NPC vendor of last resort increases the money supply, and it provides a ceiling or a floor to the market" (Sam Lewis, in Koster's notes [K2]). Here it gives the floor without the new money: a city pays from its own purse.
- **A standing order to buy exists** (`public.market_requests`, 0288:92): name a good, a price and an amount at your home city. Today the houses fill it. With more players, a player's sale should fill it first: one hunk, later.
- **Why not an exchange like RuneScape's.** It matches players to players and needs both. With one player it is an empty hall. Its job is done here by the cities and the houses. If the world fills up, the standing order grows into it; nothing is thrown away.
- **The watch.** Selling is a pipe from the cities' purses to the players. With one company it is a trickle. Before a hundred companies dig, read what the cities hold.

**When the world is nearly empty.** Production has one company that plays (content plan 2.1). So:

| Shared thing | Alone | With others |
|---|---|---|
| Sites | They work in full. You see yourself there. | You see who digs where. Up to +10 %. |
| Works | The bar still rises, from your turns and the trickle. | It rises faster, for all. |
| Rich veins | They come by the calendar whoever is there. | Everyone goes; the bonus for company stacks on it. |
| Market | Cities and houses buy and sell. | Players move the price, and fill each other's orders. |
| Boards | Your own line and the next level. | A race. |
| A boss | Its health is sized by the companies seen in the last week. One company can bring it down in a day or two of visits. | It falls faster. |

Nothing above is switched on by a crowd, and nothing is closed without one.

**People who are not players.** Cities and merchant houses stand in where it is true: they are the market. I would not paint merchant crews onto the sites as a show. If house crews are wanted, they should dig and sell for real, by a rule worked out from the clock (no rows), and that is its own slice.

## 7. What it costs the database

The rule is kept by construction: **there is no land job.** No cron, no trigger on a busy table, nothing added to the arrivals beat. A company that does not press writes nothing.

**Rows a press** are in 6.5: 1 for a Send, 4 to 6 for a Collect, later up to 9.

**Rows a day and a minute** (J). A light day is two visits: about 6 presses, 20 rows. A keen day is 30 presses: about 120 rows.

| Companies playing | Rows a day | Rows a minute, on average | In a busy hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 to 120 | under 0.1 | under 1 |
| 100 (70 light, 30 keen) | about 5,000 | about 3.5 | about 15 |
| 1,000 (700 light, 300 keen) | about 50,000 | about 35 | about 150 |

For scale: the market's price drift alone rewrites about 10,400 rows a minute (`docs/WORK_QUEUE.md`, 0420). A thousand companies on land are about a third of one per cent of that one job.

**The tables.**

| Table | Rows | Does it grow? |
|---|---|---|
| `land_sites`, `land_actions`, `land_drops`, `land_foes` | The catalogue: tens to a few hundred | No |
| `land_parties` | One a party; a company has 1 to 3 | No |
| `party_gear` | At most 3 a party | No |
| `land_marks` (later) | One a company a job it has done | Bounded by the catalogue |
| `land_site_state` (later) | One a site | No |
| `land_site_week` (later) | One a company a site a week; 8 weeks kept | Bounded |
| `level_xp` (exists) | One a company a skill | Bounded |
| `level_book` (exists, append-only) | One a skill a Collect | **Yes. The one to watch.** |

**`level_book`, said plainly.** Its bound today is the limit a day (0411:232). A land skill has no limit a day, so it needs another bound, and this is it:
- experience is booked at a Collect, at most one row a skill;
- a Collect that would book under 10 experience carries it to the next one (it waits on the party's row), so even a player who presses every minute writes at most about 8 rows an hour;
- a first time is one row, once ever: at most the catalogue's size a company;
- typical: 4 to 20 rows a company a day. At 1,000 companies that is about 10,000 small rows a day, about 1 MB.
The daily health check should list its size. If the world reaches that size, old land rows of the book are folded into one: its own slice, named now so it is not a surprise.

**The processor.**
- A dig or a make: arithmetic, and one roll a turn for rare finds: at most about 2,000 short hashes for 12 hours, in one statement.
- A hunt: one engine call a bout. The engine measured about 5 ms a fight in the local database (`docs/design/tick-combat.md` 10). At most 200 bouts a press (`land_bouts_a_press`): about a second at the very worst, once or twice a day a company. **The builder measures this on the real database before a foe is lit.**

**Reads.** `world.land()` reads the party's row, the home city's stored goods, the company's levels and the small catalogue. It is asked when the tab opens, after a press, and when the tab is shown again. It is not on the client's 15-second beat. Who is at each site is one count over `land_parties`, a table of one row a company.

**Many skills cost no more.** A company has one party doing one job, whether the game has three skills or thirty. A new skill adds rows to the catalogue and one `level_xp` row a company that trains it. Rows a day follow presses, not skills.

**The shared rows.** A site's works is one row a site. At 1,000 companies it is updated about 10,000 times a day, each a moment's lock on one row of a one-page table.

## 9. My honest judgment

**Is this the right next thing?** It is the right second thing. Not instead of the content plan's three small fixes.

**What it fixes of the content plan's four reasons.**

| Reason | Does land fix it? |
|---|---|
| 1. One loop, and the card plays it for you | **Yes.** A second loop, and no card can play it: what the party does next depends on what you are building toward. |
| 2. Nothing can go wrong | **Weakly.** A beaten party loses half a haul and ten minutes. No ship, coin or gear is at risk. Wild sites and wear raise it later. |
| 3. Money buys nothing | **Partly.** Mules, room and a second party are real sinks, in the tens of thousands. They do not replace the yard's order, which is the big one. |
| 4. No reason to come back, nobody to beat | **Yes to the first.** A haul is always waiting and the next unlock is always named. **No to the second** while one company plays: a board of one is not a race. |

**What it does not fix.** The sea. A voyage is still one choice and a wait; pirates still pass a company by; the second ship is still an errand. A new player still lands on Trade first. If land is built instead of slices A, B and C of the content plan, the game has two loops and the first one is still hollow.

**What it adds that nothing else on the list does.** Something to do during the 84 % of the first hour that is spent at sea with nothing to press (content plan 3.3). The two loops fill each other's waits. That is the strongest reason to build it.

**The risk of two half-games, and how I would hold it off.**
1. **Ship A, B and C anyway.** They are a day each, they touch other functions, and they wake systems that are built. L1 can be built beside them: it is new tables, two small hunks and one loosened check.
2. **A skill ships only when something uses what it makes.** Jagex's own numbers after its rework: mining rose, smithing did not, and ore lost its worth [R5]. Twenty skills where three matter is the same failure, self-made.
3. **L1 is the test.** It is a day and a half. If the owner, who is the one player, does not look in on his party the next day, stop there. Nothing else was spent.
4. **Keep it in the world.** Warcraft's garrisons, a game inside the game, were faulted for "isolating and detracting from the social aspect of the game world" [K5]. That is one more reason for shared sites and the shared market, and against a sealed land.
5. **Do not build for a crowd that is not here.** Who is at a site, the bonus for company, boards and works cost almost nothing, because they are read from rows that exist anyway. A boss, groups and an exchange wait for players.

**What I would cut, and have.** A map to walk. Veins that run out. An exchange. Trade in gear. Grades of quality. Pets. Merchant crews painted on the sites. A party of several players. Level 99. Heat, stamina and anything else that needs a click to stay efficient. Every skill with no user for what it makes.

**Three things the owner should know before saying yes.**
- **The numbers are small beside Melvor's.** A unit a minute, not an ore every three seconds, because a unit here is real cargo with a real price. The feel is an expedition, not a clicker.
- **Storage will fill.** A night of iron is more than a small city's Storage holds (150 tons a tier, 0070:89; iron is 0.8 tons a unit). Nothing is lost, but the party stops until bars are made or goods are sold. I count that as a choice. He may count it as a nuisance; then room becomes an early purchase (L9 moves up).
- **The catalogue's prices were not made for making.** At base values an Iron Bar is worth less than the iron and coal in it (section 11). Smithing is for use, not for coin, until those values are looked at.

**A different shape, if the owner means his second message strictly.**

| | Shape A (this document) | Shape B: a sealed land |
|---|---|---|
| What the party gets | The world's goods, in the Storage and markets that exist | Its own things: ore, bars, fish, gear, in its own store |
| "Nothing to do with ships" | No ship is ever needed. But iron is iron: a ship may carry what the party dug. | Nothing from the land can ever be in a ship or a market |
| Numbers | Small: cargo units | Melvor's: an ore every few seconds, thousands in store. Bronze first. |
| Names, prices, recipes | There already: 342 raw goods, 287 recipes, two languages | All written anew |
| A player economy | The markets and houses that exist; works with one player | An exchange to build, empty until there is a crowd |
| What it adds to the code | One list of jobs | A second catalogue, a second store and a second recipe table beside the ones that exist |

The frame is the same in both: jobs, one settle, one screen, the same Levels. What differs is what a job gives. **I recommend A.** It is less to build, it makes the catalogue the owner ordered in October into a game, and it is one world. B is the right choice only if the owner wants the land's numbers to fly, or wants no land thing ever to touch a ship. If he does, say so before L1: the choice is made there.
