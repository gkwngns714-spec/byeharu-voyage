## A. The game in ten lines

1. You are a trading **company** with a **home city**, the city you were founded in. Your **HQ** is there, and so are your
   **warehouse** and your **estates**. You hold a **Rank**.
2. You never sail a ship by hand to earn money. You **hire people** (captains, and crew for them) and **assign them work** at HQ.
3. There are three kinds of work:
   - **sea trade**: a fleet buys a named good at A, then sells it at B or brings it home to the warehouse;
   - **land work**: a party digs or makes raw goods somewhere on land;
   - **estate work**: a mine or farm at HQ produces goods for the company.
4. Every assignment shows **+/- coin per hour** before you press Assign, and again while it runs. The **Ledger** adds every line, plus
   and minus, into one **net per hour**: income, wages, maintenance, tax, dock fees, stores, repairs and dividends.
5. **The decision** is choosing between real alternatives with the same people:
   - this captain on the Lisbon-Setubal iron run at +4,100/h, or in the home mine at +1,300/h;
   - sell the cork at Bristol for coin now, or bring it home for the yard;
   - a third ship, a second party, or an estate.

   The best option changes as prices, glut, levels and upkeep move. Finding it is the skill (EVE's market eye, Melvor's idle loop).
6. **First 10 minutes:** your HQ shows one fleet idle and one captain. Assign "Iron · Lisbon → Setubal · Sell", see +X/h, and press.
   Then assign the land party to Iron at home. Watch the Ledger turn green.
7. **First hour:** the fleet runs by itself. Trade levels rise and the first Rank step opens a second captain slot. You hire a captain at
   the Inn, compare two trades, and re-assign. You see the first maintenance line and the dividend from a small share.
8. **First week:** three fleets and two parties. A home mine estate is staffed by crew. Gold dug at Mining 60 is sold or kept.
   Higher Rank opens farther seas and lands, and cuts tax. Skills cut upkeep. You re-plan twice a day from the Ledger's per-hour lines.
9. **Risk is real but never strands you.** Pirates, glut and upkeep can make a line red. The home region and enough paying work are
   always open at Rank 1.
10. **The Map is where you look; HQ is where you act.** No other screen commits work.

## A2. The core loop is Melvor Idle's, run with many characters

The owner, 2026-11-24: *"it is like madevor's rs3 jagex game idle game, but instead of doing with only 1 player, i do it with multiple -
get it?"* (Melvor Idle, the Jagex-published idle game built on RuneScape's skills; research: `docs/research/games-2026-11-24/melvor-idle.md`,
`runescape-3.md`.)

**The loop (Melvor's, unchanged).**
1. A worker is set to ONE action.
2. The action repeats on its own timer.
3. Each completion gives goods or coin, plus XP.
4. Levels open better actions.
5. It keeps running while you are away.

**The difference.** Melvor has one character doing one thing. Here you run MANY workers at once, each on her own action, all on one
screen. **The game is choosing who does what.** A worker is one "character slot": a fleet with her crew (and her captain, if one is
seated), a land party, or an estate. A captain makes the worker she leads better (A2.3), and that is the reason a choice of who goes
where is interesting.

Sea trade runs, land harvesting, estate work and making goods (smithing) are all the same thing: an ACTION with an interval, inputs,
outputs, XP and a cost, performed by a worker.

### A2.1 The action: one shape for every kind of work

Every option a worker can take is served as one row of one shape. The client draws the row; it never computes it.

| Field | Sea trade run | Land job (party) | Estate job | Make job (smithing) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kind` | `sea` | `gather` | `gather` (or `make`) at home | `make` |
| `skill` | Trading (and Exploration for miles) | the job's skill (Mining) | the job's skill | Smithing |
| `level` needed | 1, plus the area tier's Rank | `land_actions.level`, plus the region's tier | `land_actions.level`, plus the estate's tier | `land_actions.level` |
| `interval_s` | one whole cycle: BUY at A + sail out + SELL or STORE at B + sail back to A, with the dock turns (`port_prep_seconds` each) | the trip: march + turns x the job's seconds + march (+ passage both ways for a far land) | the job's seconds x the turns a cycle (no march) | the recipe's seconds |
| `inputs` | the coin for the units bought (the quote) | none | none for gather; the recipe's goods from the Warehouse for make | the recipe's goods from the Warehouse |
| `outputs` | the sale's coin (`end = 'sell'`), or the units into the Warehouse at their basis (`end = 'store'`, 0492) | units of the good into the home Warehouse | units into the home Warehouse | units into the Warehouse |
| `xp` per completion | Trading points (1 per 500 coin bought or sold) + Exploration (1 per 100 nm, 25 a new harbour) | the job's `xp` x the turns | the same | the recipe's `xp` |
| `cost` per completion | tax + dock fee + wages + stores (the 0490 sell figures: `tax`, `wages`, `dock_fee`) | wages ashore (0498) | wages ashore + estate decay (0499) | none beyond wages |
| derived, served | `per_hour` = net coin x 3600 / interval; `xp_per_hour`; `items_per_hour` | the same three | the same three | the same three |

- **It is data, not code per kind.** The shape is ONE served read, `world.actions(worker)`, built in 0495 from the server's existing
  quotes:
  - sea: `world.good_board` / `world.best_sales` (profit, seconds), the same rows the desk and the board already read;
  - land and estate: `public.land_terms` (`world.land`);
  - make: the recipe rows.

  Catalogue actions (land and make) are rows of `land_actions`. A sea action is generated from the market (good x buy x sell), because
  a trade is a market answer, not a catalogue row. Both come out in the one shape.
- **The chosen action is stored on the worker:**
  - a fleet's `fleet_jobs` row: `best`, or `fixed {good, buy, sell, end, times}`;
  - a party's or an estate's `land_parties` row: `action` and the `terms` frozen at Send.

  There is no third record.

### A2.2 What a "tick" is: the server owns time

There is **no client simulation**. A completion is a server event:
- **Sea:** the cycle completes at its SELL (the `SOLD` event and its `trip_net`) or its STORE. The 2-minute job tick
  (`public.tick_fleet_jobs`, 0481) starts the next cycle when the fleet lies idle in port. The voyage beat settles the sail.
- **Land and estate:** `public.land_settle` books the turns that have come home from the time that has passed (0455), at Collect, at a
  read, and at the land-carry job's collect.

The progress bar on a worker's row is drawn from served stamps only:
- sea: the voyage's start and `due_at`, plus which leg of the cycle she is on;
- land: `started_at` plus the frozen terms' trip seconds.

The client draws the bar between those stamps. That is drawing, not a second clock.

### A2.3 Skills: held by the COMPANY; captains make a worker better

**Decided by default (OWNER'S):** skill **levels are the company's**. There is one ladder per skill, and every worker benefits from it.
**A captain carries her own quality**, which makes the worker she leads better at one kind of work. That quality is her grade, her
traits, her captain skills and her own levels.

Why, from the code:
- **Company levels are what exists.** `player_progress` and `level_xp` / `level_book` hold the 11 sea levels and Mining. Every
  reader reads them: `level_perks` / `level_perk` (0417, 0468), `title_level` (Rank), `land_actions.level`, the Academy and the yard.
  Moving levels onto captains would re-cut every reader, and a dismissed captain would take the company's progress with her.
- **Captain quality also exists, as a per-worker multiplier:**
  - captains have grades C/B/A/S and 10 traits each, and a job trait naming her role (exploration, trading or combat);
  - they have their own three levels per signing (Exploration, Trading, Combat; `captain_level_pct_per_level` +2 % a level);
  - captain skills are bought with Reputation (0355);
  - all of it is read through ONE reader, `public.crew_pct(fleet, ship, effect, good)`.
- **That is exactly the reason to choose who does what.** A trading-job S-grade captain on the pepper run, a C-grade on the mine. This
  is RS3's Player-Owned Port model: the port's levels are yours, and captains level 0-10 on their own voyages (RS3 notes 1.5).
- **One authority each:**
  - "what is open" = the company's level (`level_xp`, `level_perks`);
  - "how well this worker does it" = `crew_pct` of her captain.

  On land, the captain's quality applies through the same `crew_pct` (0498). There is no second captain-stat system.

**The Skills list (Melvor's sidebar)** is an HQ sheet, "Skills". It is today's `/levels` page (roadmap slice 10:
`src/features/you/LevelsScreen.tsx`, `Levels.tsx`), opened in place. One tile per company skill:
- Trading, Exploration, Combat, Supplies, Weather, Shipbuilding, Requests, Craft, Routes, Investment, Evasion;
- Mining;
- Smithing (while `land_smith_enabled`);
- Farming, once its rows exist.

Each tile shows the level, an XP bar, and **the actions it offers by level**:
- land and make skills: the `land_actions` rows, with locked rows marked with their level;
- Trading: the `level_perks` openings and the area tiers;
- Shipbuilding: the hulls the Academy opens.

Rank (the Title) heads the list.

### A2.4 Mastery: per action, on the one goods mastery

Melvor gives each action a mastery that grows with repeating that action. Here that is **goods mastery (0479)**:
- table `public.goods_mastery`: one row per company and good, levels 1-10 by units;
- the reader is `public.goods_mastery_level`;
- the price benefit is read through `world.quote_terms`, the allowance through `world.daily_cap_remaining`.

It is extended, not copied. **No second mastery table.**
- **The action's key is its good.** The same route sells the same good; the same mineral is the good dug; the same recipe makes the
  same good. Per good, not per route: a route mastery would be a second key on the same ladder.
- **More things count** (0501). The units of the good completed by ANY action count toward its mastery:
  - sold, as today;
  - stored home by a sea run;
  - dug by a party or an estate;
  - made by a recipe.
- **What a level gives, by kind.** These are Melvor's checkpoints, as additive steps on the base value with floors and caps (melvor
  notes section 1):
  - **sea:** the existing +0.5 % sale price a level and +5 % day's allowance a level (0479), unchanged;
  - **gather:** +1 % chance a level of a second unit (cap 10 %, at 10) and -1 % job seconds a level (cap -10 %);
  - **make:** +1 % chance a level to keep the inputs ("preserve", cap 10 %) and +1 % chance of a second unit (cap 10 %).

  The step sizes are knobs of the 0479 family. They are applied in the one place each figure is computed (`land_terms` for seconds,
  the turn's roll for doubles, `forge_take` for preserve).
- **Logbook sets (0478)** stay the collection log: one-time Reputation and a small benefit per finished set. They are NOT turned into a
  mastery pool. A pool would be a second mastery currency; that is OWNER'S: decided by default (no pool).

### A2.5 Offline: Melvor's rule, on the server

- Progress continues while away because every completion is the server's (A2.2). Online and offline follow the same rules.
- **One cap for every worker: the existing knob `land_away_hours` (12)**. It is read as "the away cap" by:
  - the land party (as today);
  - estates;
  - the fleet job tick: no NEW cycle starts after the cap since her last sign-in; the cycle under way finishes.

  Melvor's figure is 24 h; raising the knob to 24 is the owner's (it is an existing knob). The economy file section 11 is re-pointed to
  this one knob.
- **Charges pause too.** Hourly upkeep and wages pause after the same cap (economy file section 11), so a dormant company is never
  drained.
- **Return: "While you were away", per worker.** One row per worker: her action, the completions, units made or sold, coin +/-, XP
  gained and levels crossed, and the reason she stopped (cap, full shed, a refusal). Then the totals.
  - It is `world.away_report` (0481), extended in 0495 to land parties and estates.
  - It is the one away read. The device-local `app/AwayCard.tsx` is deleted with it.


### A2.6 Crew slots are the growth: bought at a total level, with gold and Reputation

Melvor grows one character's levels. **Here the main growth is the NUMBER of workers.**

The owner, 2026-11-24: *"crew slot will be open when total level hits, with gold, reputation rquirement"*.

A **crew slot** is one worker: a fleet with work, a land party or an estate, each with her crew and (at sea, optionally) her captain.

**A slot opens when three things are met, and then the player BUYS it with one press.**
1. **Total level.** The sum of the company's skill levels (Melvor's / RuneScape's "total level"). One server reader,
   `public.total_level(player)`, sums the same levels the Skills sheet lists (A2.3), from `level_xp` / `player_progress`:
   - the eleven sea levels (1-20 each);
   - every land skill that is lit (`public.land_skill_on`): Mining, then Smithing and Farming when lit (1-100 each).

   Levels are the company's (A2.3), so the total is the company's, never the captains'. Rank (the Title) is not added: it is derived
   from Trading and Exploration, which are already counted. At founding the total is 12 (11 sea + Mining 1).
2. **Gold.** A price paid once at the press, to the home city by `pay_city` (kind `CREW_SLOT`). It is moved to a city, never destroyed:
   the closed economy holds.
3. **Reputation.** **Spent**, not only shown. Reputation is already a spendable balance: captain skill steps spend it through
   `public.reputation_credit`, the one place the balance falls (0355). A threshold-only rule would make it a second kind of number.
   OWNER'S: decided by default.

**The ladder** (OWNER'S: default, reversible; one data table, `public.crew_slot_steps(slot, total_level, price, reputation)`). It is
sized from the economy file's measured denominators:
- Trading L2 in 3 min, L5 in 48 min, L10 in 4 h;
- Mining L10 in 27 min, L20 in ~1.5 h, L30 in ~3.5 h;
- Exploration +1 a few new harbours early;
- income ~10,000 / h at the start, ~30,000 at Trading 5, ~75,000 at 10, ~200,000 at 15, ~450,000 at 20;
- Reputation, measured from its rules: a near sale gives about profit / 250, so ~15 a run and ~90 / h from trade, plus 10 x the level
  for each level crossed, plus 25 a new harbour. That is roughly 250-400 in the first hour; the capped kinds stop at `rep_day_cap`
  600 a real day.

| Slot | Total level | Gold | Reputation (spent) | When a steady player meets it | Gold against income then |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (start) | 0 | 0 | at founding | |
| 2 | 16 | 5,000 | 50 | ~10-20 min (Trading 3, Exploration 2, Mining 3) | ~30 min of income |
| 3 | 30 | 20,000 | 250 | ~1-1.5 h | ~1 h |
| 4 | 45 | 60,000 | 600 | ~4-6 h | ~2 h at ~30 k/h |
| 5 | 65 | 150,000 | 1,200 | ~1 day | ~2 h at ~75 k/h |
| 6 | 90 | 400,000 | 2,500 | ~3 days | ~2 h at ~200 k/h |
| 7 | 120 | 1,000,000 | 5,000 | ~1 week | ~2 h at ~450 k/h |
| 8 | 160 | 2,500,000 | 10,000 | ~2-3 weeks | ~5 h |

- **One cap on workers.** The bought slot count is the one cap on how many workers run at once. Fleet room's FLEET purchase
  (`cmd.buy_room('fleet')`, 0354) folds into it; `fleet_hard_max` 6 and ships-per-fleet room stay as they are.
- `party_slots` (Mining) and `estate_slots` (Rank) in `level_perks` still bound how many of each kind may fill a slot.
- **Existing companies keep every worker they have.** At deploy each company's slot count starts at no less than the workers it holds,
  and no less than the most any company holds now.
- **On the HQ worker list:**
  - the next slot stands as a **locked row** at the foot of the list, with three bars: total level n / N, gold n / N, Reputation n / N;
  - when all three are met it shows **Buy**;
  - a refusal names the requirement in the server's words. Nothing is refused silently.
- **Dark** behind `crew_slots_enabled` (in 0497). Until it is lit, nothing is capped, and H1 lists the fleets and the party that exist.
- The economy file's `captain_slots` row (on Rank) is **replaced** by this ladder.

## B. The model: one authority each

### B1. Worker: Captain, Crew

**Exists:**
- `public.officers` and `player_officers`, with `captain_state`, grades C/B/A/S, traits, captain skills (Reputation) and promotion.
  - Hire: `cmd.hire_officer`, which pays OFFICER_WAGE once, to the city.
  - Seat: `cmd.seat_captain` / `cmd.post_officer`.
- Crew: a count per hull (`ships.crew`), hired at an Inn with `cmd.do_hire` (`hire_crew_rate` per head) and let go with `cmd.do_dismiss`.
  - Crew wages accrue per sea-day (`wage_per_crew_day` x `running_cost_level`, `fleet_wages.owed`, at most 30 days).
  - They are charged only at a sale: `public.wages_due`, from the profit, never on a loss.

**Becomes:**
- A **Worker** is a captain, with the crew under her.
- A captain is **seated in a fleet** (as today) or **leads a land party** or **runs an estate** (new). One seat at a time; the seat is
  the assignment's.
- Crew stay a count on a hull for ships. For a party or an estate, crew is a count on that party or estate row (new, B4).

**Renamed (client):**
- They are shown as the rows of the one worker list (C1): each row is a worker on one action (A2).
- Hiring stays at the Inn, which opens in place on HQ (S1).

**New:**
- **Crew slots**, bought at a total level with gold and Reputation (A2.6).
- **Wages ashore** for party and estate crew, through the same wage reader (`public.crew_wages`). Ashore there is no sale to take
  them from, so they are charged hourly (B4).

**Deleted:** nothing.

### B2. Asset: Fleet, Warehouse, Estate

**Fleet. Exists:**
- `fleets` / `ships`, with roles (0485), fleet room (0354; `fleet_hard_max` 6), and `cmd.move_ship` / `rename_fleet`.
- State is read only through `public.fleet_state_of` (CLAUDE.md).

Unchanged. Fleets stay a tab: the owner kept it in S7.

**Warehouse. Exists:**
- `public.player_storage(player, port, good)`, one shed per harbour, with capacity `storage_cap(port)` = the city's warehouse tier x 150 t.
- Doors: `STORE` / `TAKE` (0070), the forge (`cmd.forge_good`, 0281/0461), and Land output (`storage_put` at the party's home).

**Becomes:**
- **The Warehouse is the shed at the home city.** HQ shows it as one bar (used / cap) and its goods.
- Sheds elsewhere stay, as rows "Stored elsewhere".
- Goods still move only by ship.
- The NO-HOME law of `docs/FLEETS_AND_SLOTS.md` §1 ("there is no home base") is superseded by the owner's 2026-11-24 order: the
  company has a home. The physics stays: nothing moves between harbours without a ship. `public.home_port_of(player)` (0288, the
  founding port) is the one reading of "home".

**Estate. New, but not a new production engine:**
- An **Estate** is a building at HQ, such as a Mine or (later) a Farm, that runs **one land job of the home region forever**.
- Its rules are the Land frame's own rules:
  - the job: `land_actions`;
  - the site: `land_sites`, the home region's ground;
  - the settle: `land_settle`;
  - the haul: `storage_put` into the home Warehouse;
  - the market guard: 0457.
- What makes it an estate rather than a party:
  - it never marches, so a turn is the job's seconds alone;
  - it is staffed by a captain + crew;
  - it has a tier (bought) that sets its crew seats;
  - it pays upkeep.
- **Farm** needs the Farming skill rows: the `fields` ground and its `land_actions`, a data slice, as the land doc §5.1 planned.
  Until those rows exist only the **Mine** is offered.
- **Gold mine** = the Mine on the `gold` / `gold-dust` jobs (Mining 60), where the home region holds them.

The rules that tie them together:
- **The concept is one, the records are per worker.** A fleet holds at most one assignment (its `fleet_jobs` row). A party or an estate

- **One door per kind.** Sea work goes through `cmd.set_fleet_job`; land and estate work through `cmd.land_send`. The area gate (B5)

### B4. Ledger: income and every cost line, as a RATE

**Exists:**
- `public.ledger(player, kind, ducats_delta, fleet_id, created_at)`. The only money writer is `public.credit`; `pay_city` and
  `city_pays` are the city legs. The closed economy's `mint_guard` refuses new coin.

**Cost kinds that exist today:**

| Kind | What it is |
|---|---|
| TAX | Profit tax `profit_tax_at`: `tax_rate_default` 3 %, at most 20 %, x the country stance (home 0.25, ally 0.5, rival/war 2) |
| PORT_FEE | Dock fee: base + 0.5 % of the cargo, x country / regard / logbook / home -50 % |
| WAGES | Per sea-day, charged at the sale |
| PROVISION | Stores |
| REPAIR | Repairs |
| HIRE / OFFICER_WAGE | Hiring crew / a captain's signing fee |
| BUILD | The yard |
| INSURANCE | Insurance |
| LAND | Picks and mules |
| SHARE / DONATION | Investing and gifts |

There is **no maintenance, no storage fee, no building upkeep, no loan**. Today every cost is per event.

**The rate exists in one place:** `world.hq_board().rate`. It is `{per_hour: round(sum(ledger, last 24 h) / 24), net_24h}`, with
FOUNDING and probes left out (0480). The bridge shows it as "+X / hour" when the board is served. When the board is dark, v1 divides

- The economy is closed. `economy_closed` and the city purses are lit on prod. `public.mint_guard` refuses every "minted" kind at
